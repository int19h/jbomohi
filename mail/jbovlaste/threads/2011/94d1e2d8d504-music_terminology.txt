Nitcion started this off many years ago, and proposed 
a host of terms on: http://www.lojban.org/tiki/Music+terminology

Here I some really basic ones I would like to add to the list.  The 
fu'ivla are probably all wrong, so please offer suggestions.

Classical music: nolzgi
Folk music: naizgi
Pop music:    ninzgi
Jazz: dja'aza
Symphony: sinfana  
Concerto: konceto
Composer: zgifi'i
Conductor: zgitro

1) nolzgi
z1=n1 the classical music performed at/by z2 (event) originating in culture n1
from
z1=n1 zgike z2 gi'e nobli n2
 
2)naizgi
z1 naizgi is folk/ethnic music performed at/by z2 (event), originating in group 
of people n2
from
z1 zgike z2 ra'i lo natmi be n2
 
3) ninzgi
z1=n1 the pop music performed at/by z2 (event)
 
4) dja'aza
d1 is jazz music performed at/by z2 (event)
 
5) sinfana
x1 is a symphony composed by x1 consisting of movements x3
 
6)konceto
x1 is a concerto composed by x2 consisting of movements x3 played with solo 
instruments x4
 
7) zgifi'i
f1 composes music f2=z1 for function/purpose f3=z2 from existing elements/ideas 
f4from
f1 finti f2=z1 poi zgike z2 ku'o f3 f4
 
8) zgitro 
j1 conducts music z1 being performed by/at z2=j3 (event)
from
j1 jitro lonu z1 zgike z2=j3

I agree with "simfoni". In many languages, including Greek where the word 
comes from, a nasal consonant assimilates to a following "f". The Japanese ン, 
though transliterated "n", is a generic nasal. Spanish writes "sinfonía", 
where the "n" is pronounced as a labiodental. Lojban does distinguish 
between "nf" and "mf", and should write "mf" in this word. (So does 
English: "unfounded" versus "dumbfounded" is an example.)

Btw, the Aramaic word סומפניה (sumponyah) in Daniel 3:5 looks like a borrowing 
from the same Greek word.
