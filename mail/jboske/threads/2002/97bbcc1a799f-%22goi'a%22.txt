The principle in this case is that an elidable terminator selma'o ought
not to have more than one cmavo in it, because then it is impossible to
reconstruct what cmavo should appear when filling in elisions. This
was discovered back when TO and TOI had two members each, one meaning ( ) and
one meaning [ ], before sa'a was devised.

=== 6 | 2002-12-18T17:21:35Z | John Cowan <cowan@ccil.org> | 200212181734.maa09574@mail2.reutershealth.com | mail/jboske/cur/1040232095.3126d8e91804db67.jbomohi:2,S
And Rosta scripsit:

> Fair enough. But all the parser needs to know is the selmaho, no?

Well, in one sense. But the whole idea of *elidable* terminators is
that they can be elided because one (a parser or a human being) can
reconstruct the unique (sequence of) terminators that are missing.
If there is more than one cmavo in an elidable-terminator selma'o, this
is not possible: does

le broda [] cu brode

mean

le broda ku cu brode

or

le broda goi'a cu brode
