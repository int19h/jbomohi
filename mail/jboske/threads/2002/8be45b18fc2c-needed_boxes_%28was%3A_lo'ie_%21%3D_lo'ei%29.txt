It definitely doesn't do "any". I think "loi" probably can
though---the "sundry unattached body parts" objections people have
raised don't break it because of grice. "I need any white horse"
== "mi nitcu loi blabi xirma".

The objections to {loi} for this function are not about body
parts. {loi} works just like {lo} for the purposes of intension.
{mi nitcu loi blabi xirma} means "there is some fraction of the
mass of all white horses such that I need that fraction". That
the fractions may be made up of whole horses or not is not
relevant to the objection.

You may be reading {loi} as the Unique of fractions though,
which is consistent with some parts of CLL. In that case, it
works, because Unique does work here.

Can someone remind me why "I need a doctor" isn't



mi nitcu ledu'u so'uda cu mikce mi



Similarly "I need a box" as



(de zo'u) mi nitcu ledu'u (da zo'u) da tanxe de

That works if {nitcu} means "x1 needs x2 to be true
(so that x3 is also true)".

Do you mean to propose that to be the only way to use
{nitcu}, or as an extension of its basic meaning? In
other words, could we still say:

mi nitcu le va tanxe le du'u setca lei vi cukta ty
I need that box to put these books in.

?



If we can say that, then it is useful to also be able
to say:

mi nitcu lo'e tanxe le du'u setca lei vi cukta ty
I need a box to put these books in.

which is not exactly the same as:



mi nitcu le du'u da tanxe lei vi cukta
I need that these books are in a box.
