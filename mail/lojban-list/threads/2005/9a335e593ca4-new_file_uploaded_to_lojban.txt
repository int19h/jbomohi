=== 3 | 2005-02-25T15:54:50Z | Matt Arnold <mattarn@123.net> | 200502251554.j1pfsotd022480@mole.e-mol.com | mail/lojban-list/cur/1109346890.78d8b3c8cec310ec.jbomohi:2,S

The publications of LLG have been wonderful and very well-done. But it deserves better desktop publishing and illustration than it has received. I would be very pleased to volunteer my time, talent and software. I've posted a nineteen-page sample PDF to the files section.
I would especially direct the attention of the group to the last example on page 19 {ta [cu] tavla be do be le melbi [ku] [be'o] vecnu} to see the way the diagram tables can nest. I was only beginning to learn Lojban two years ago when I made this document, and it helped my understanding greatly to draw out the diagram.
As you see, selbri cells have a black border, sumti cells have a white border. I gave place marker cmavo a slash through the cell. Question cmavo were given an x. Negation cmavo are white letters on a black cell. Someone with a more sophisticated understanding of the grammer could probably suggest a lot of improvements to the illustration.
.filip.,
I could change the apostrophes, if it's useful to do so. It would take a lot of work but legibility and good typography was what this effort was all about.
-epkat

I don't know what the consensus is, or even whether there is one. I
was merely reminded of this issue, which had come up before, I believe
when some flyers were drawn up to be handed out at conventions to
advertise Lojban, and there was a bit of discussion as the "best"
shape of that character.

I suspect my preference is largely an artefact of the fonts I've used
to see Lojban in, and of the fact that ASCII apostrophe, as used on
most web pages, is typically fairly straight, so I've never seen much
"good Lojban typography". I can imagine that there are no rules of
typography beyond those that knowledgeable people will impose when
they start to typeset Lojban with a bit of care and attention.
