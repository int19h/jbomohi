Perhaps we could introduce a special symbol to indicate
that what follows is a conventional *written* abbreviation 
of something that has to be expanded when read aloud.

always be read as "milylitce be li". Using {ml} by itself
could be confused with the name {ml.}, and some other units

Actually, the morphology proposed in
<http://www.lojban.org/tiki/tiki-index.php?page=PEG+Morphology+Algorithm>
provides rafsi for every single brivla, including those that start with
a vowel. The non-initial rafsi for fu'ivla that start with vowel simply
add a '. So if the fu'ivla was {.enri}, the lujvo for milli- and micro-henry 
would be {mily'enri} and {miky'enri}.

A unit of inductance.  When the current through a one-henry coil changes by 
one amp per second, the voltage across the coil is one volt.

Personal name units are a pain in the butt.  I've thought about translating 
them as personal names (la henry) with some kind of tag on them.  

Do we have the "sverdrup"?  That's 1e15 cubic meters per second, a 
reasonably scaled unit for ocean currents named after a founder of that 
field.  I would deprecate all the scaled units like this one, and make 
everyone use E format, e.g. "the flux of North Atlantic Deep Water was 8e15 
M^3/s before Bush's environmental policies pushed..."

How about using a hyphen but at the beginning of the abbreviation?
That way it is easier to parse, and besides the hyphen at the end I 
would reserve for splitting a word at the end of a line that continues
in the next line.

I would also include the "be li" as part of the abbreviation, so we
can say both {le karce cu -Mg 2} and {lo -Mg 2 cu dekto lo -kg 2},
without having to write any words between the unit and the number.
The abbreviations could not be used without a number, but 
that is the normal usage in other languages too, we can write
"1 kg" but not really "a kg".
