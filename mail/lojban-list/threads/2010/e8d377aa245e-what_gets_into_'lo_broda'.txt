Well, now I am confused yet again.  In my rethinking, I figured out that lo cimei be lo broda would be a set of things that realized 'cimei be lo broda',

I would say "things that" instead of "a set of things that", but ok.

that is, for every thing among lo cimei lo broda ther are two other things

and no more than two other things...

among lo cimei lo broda and thus all three are among lo broda.  So lo cimei be lo broda must have at least three members but might have any number at all greater than that.

At least three and at most three.

ko'a cimei .ijo ko'a su'ocimei gi'e su'ecimei
"A are three iff A are at least three and at most three."

 Now it would seem that lo ci lo broda, since it has an internal quantifier does have exactly three members of lo broda, so it is not the same.

The internal "quantifier" tells which N-mei predicate the referents of
the sumti satisfy. So if the internal "quantifier" is "ci", the
referents of that sumti satisfy "cimei".

On Mon, Apr 26, 2010 at 1:44 PM, John E. Clifford <kali9putra@yahoo.com> wrote:
> Oops! I forgot that the exactitude of 'ci' would come into play.  So, if we want two trios, what do we do? 'lo re cimei'?

lo re ci mei are actually 23.

"lo reboi cimei" are both two and three: they are three because they
satisfy "zo'e cimei" and they are two because that's what the inner
"re" says, so they also satisfy "zo'e remei". My guess is that in this
particular case the "inner quantifier" forces the reification of the
three into one, because otherwise it is redundant to count again what
is already counted by its very definition. But if three can only be
three, then "lo reboi cimei" is just like the square circle.

When more clarity is desirable, then "lo re gunma be lo cimei": "the
two groups of three" is better, as now it is clear that it's the
groups that are two, not the things that are three.

mu'o mi'e xorxes
