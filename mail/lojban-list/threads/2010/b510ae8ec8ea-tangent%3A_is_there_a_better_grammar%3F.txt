On Wed, Apr 7, 2010 at 01:03, And Rosta <and.rosta@gmail.com> wrote:

> [* As I see it, the design problem has two parts. Both have to do with
> finding ways to logically precise meanings concise enough to be worth the
> effort of verbalizing. One part is to find a more concise way of of encoding
> variables than standard predicate logic notation and Lojban offer, given
> that in most propositions we express (in natural language) there are many
> variables and each variable tends to be argument of many predicates. The
> other part is to devise an inventory of predicates that expand to more
> complex logical structures.]


I don't really see your case here; if one of the basic goals is to be a
useful human language, then I don't see any alternative to predicate logic
as two-dimensional representation of utterances. Or, if there was one. it
would seem inherently illogical due to it's complexity. Could you (or
anyone) expand this thought?
