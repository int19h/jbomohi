This is a proposal for a Lojban spelling alphabet.

Properties:

* All normal letter words begin with the letter they symbolize.
* All normal letter words except five contain the letter they symbolize twice.
* No vowel pair occurs twice, except in {.alba}/{slaka}.
* All words are defined, except {.alba}, which is a nonce shortening
of {.alba'aka}.
* The words {denpa} and {slaka} match their conventional meaning.
* The word for space, {du}, is the only monosyllable.
* All consonants are represented by gismu.
* All vowels are represented by fu'ivla.
* All words are either brivla or GOhA.

   a     .alba
   b     binxo
   c     cutci
   d     dunda
   e     .estre
   g     gugde
   i     .irci
   j     jijnu
   k     kakne
   l     loldi
   m     moklu
   n     nirna
   o     .omso
   p     plipe
   r     renro
   s     sanso
   t     tcati
   u     .ulmu
   v     voksa
   x     xekri
   z     zanru
         du
   '     co'e
   ,     slaka
   .     denpa
   y     bukyvoi

Yeah, I knew about that one, and I obviously like the fact that it's
based on an international standard.  I just thought it would be nice
to have one that's more specifically suited to Lojban.

> http://www.lojban.org/tiki/tiki-index.php?page=vlaselyle'u

Didn't know about that one!  It's pretty similar to mine, but it has
six duplicated vowel pairs instead of one.

Also, it only has a few ones with the symbolized letter repeated,
and it uses several undefined words.

One advantage it has is that {kunti} for space makes a whole lot
more sense than {du}, which I chose because it's a monosyllable
that also happens to be in GOhA (although I'm not sure what the
advantage of having all the words be in GOhA is).


I'm also thinking of defining a pair of cmavo that means "treat all
these words as letterals, and parse the result as Lojban text."

I'd also like to have a way to state the text normally in parallel,
so maybe that'd be three cmavo ---

    [FOO normal text] BAR spelled-out text BAZ

--- where the "FOO normal text" part is optional.

So, let's say they're called {sa'e'u}, {lo'e'u} and {le'e'u}.  Then we
could have, for example,

   mi'e la sa'e'u daniel lo'e'u dunda alba nirna irci estre laldo le'e'u

Anyone like that?
