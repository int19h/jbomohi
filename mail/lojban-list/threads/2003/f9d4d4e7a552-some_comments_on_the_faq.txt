T2 claims that {ko bevri re spageti palta} could be mis-heard as {ko bevri
respa ge ti palta} -- but that would violate the stress rules of Lojban (not
to mention grammar -- can you have {ge} without {gi}?):

{ko bevri re spageti palta} has to be {ko BEvri re spaGEti PALta} while {ko
bevri respa ge ti palta} has to be either {ko BEvri REspa ge ti PALta} or
{ko BEvri REspa GE ti PALta}.

Specifically, {respa} has to be pronounced {REspa} but those two syllables
may not be stressed like that in the first example since, as I understand
it, cmavo before brivla may not be stressed -- {re spageti} can only be {re
spaGEti} and not {RE spaGEti} (though it could be {RE. spaGEti}).

And vlatai claims that {spageti} is a valid stage-4 fu'ivla (while not
recognising {djarspageti} as type-3 lujvo with short rafsi).

So I'm not sure that the example is that good. (I don't have a better one,
however. {pitsa} would be even worse since it's a valid gismu form. Not sure
whether there are any other good "quintessential examples", as the FAQ calls
it, of fu'ivla.)



T4: "[And Rosta ...] sometimes uses it the hopes" should probably be
"sometimes uses it *in* the hopes that ...".

You are absolutely right. There is nothing wrong with {spageti}
as a fu'ivla. If {ko bevri re spageti palta} can be misheard,
then by the same criteria {ko bevri re spati palta} can be
misheard as {ko bevri respa ti palta} (also ungrammatical).

>And vlatai claims that {spageti} is a valid stage-4 fu'ivla (while not
>recognising {djarspageti} as type-3 lujvo with short rafsi).

{djarspageti} is NOT a type-3 fu'ivla. Only consonant ending
rafsi can be used in type-3. {djarspageti} happens to be a
valid fu'ivla, but for example {djarpitsa} is not (it fails
slinku'i).
