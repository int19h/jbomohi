I would *guess* that it is simply "the origin of the dirt" - ie, te
bakykakpa would be vague and whether it means the field (which the furrows
are dug into) or the furrows would vary according to context. However, since
usage decides what lujvo place structures are, the use that is more common
will become the standard.
Note, however, that "foldi" already covers fields, so "furrow" will be more
likely to need to be said with a bakykakpa place.

Because someone else at some point in time ALSO didn't look in the gismu 
list and coined the word.

The words in NORALUJV merely include everything that I could find usage of 
up to some date, with no implication that they mean anything (indeed some 
could be other-language text that appears to be a valid lojban 
word).  Those words with higher usage numbers in the first couple of 
columns are most likely to be valid words (or obvious English words).  But 
no one has ever done any simple checks to see that the words all *should* 
mean something.  That is why ages ago I was asking for people to keyword 
the whole list first, because that would allow us to weed out words that 
might mean nothing  ("stage-copulate" aka "single", for example, was in 
there at one point, but I think I deleted it as obviously not a real lojban 
coining - not that someone might not be able to come up with a definition 
for it %^)
