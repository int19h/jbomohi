{se binxo se cusku} can mean pretty much whatever you like, given its tanru
nature, as long as the basic claim of se cusku holds.

Assuming the practical definition of binxo [x1 becomes x2 (concrete) under
conditions x3], {.i mi nelci lo se binxo be lo se cusku} expands into {.i
mi nelci zo'e noi zo'e noi cusku ke'a ku'o binxo ke'a}. That's very
strange, because the things said by someone don't change at all. You say
them, and that's it. Therefore, it's odd to talk about {lo se binxo be lo
se cusku}. {.i mi nelci lo du'u baku da'i do makau cusku} is maybe a more
exact translation of the meaning.

mu'o mi'e la tsani

Careful, Logjam terminology not withstanding, tanru are only occasionally metaphors in the extralojbanic sense. But I assume you are not working on a literal version of the English, something along the lines of {mi nelci lo nu do klama makau}, but "I like the resultant message" ( very loosely) doesn't capture it. I suppose you mean either that you like what you anticipate to be the final point of the discussion or that you lie the way that the position is being presented or (probably) both.  I suppose tense are pretty inevitably involved.
