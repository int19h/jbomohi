No language can ever be 'complete' - new words are always needed as
the world continues to change. I'm sure you've found that there is
vocabulary missing from the dictionary (I have missed a lujvo for
'bacon', for example). New lujvo will inevitably be added, and I'd be
surprised if no new gismu are ever created although this should be
rare. There are also proposals for new cmavo, and some of those will
inevitably be formally added to the language.

I do not speak officially of course, but this is the general
impression I've got while I've been studying Lojban.

Compound words are called "lujvo", and they exist.

There are a few pseudonyms ("plicme") in the dictionary, such as
"camgusmis", but I suspect you meant synonyms ("mitsmuvla").

I don't know of a lojban thesaurus, but vlasisku
(http://vlasisku.lojban.org/) and jbovlaste
(http://jbovlaste.lojban.org/) sometimes have cross-references to
similar or related words.

Is any human language *ever* completed? There are lots of concepts for which 
we don't have words yet, and sometimes the discussion of what to call them 
produces no conclusion. Here's one: a week or two ago, on the way to church, 
I saw a sun dog and explained to the guy I was riding with how a "parhelio" 
happens; he had apparently never heard this word before. (Neither had I, at 
least in Spanish. I've read about them in English.) I don't know what to call 
them in Lojban. Not "solge'u"; that is malgli (or malbro - they're 
called "kalbey hashemesh"), as it is not any kind of mammal, does not bark, 
is not carnivorous, etc.

If you'd like to contribute words to Lojban, please sign up on the jbovlaste 
website. You'll also be added to the jbovlaste mailing list, which is where 
discussions of new words happen.
