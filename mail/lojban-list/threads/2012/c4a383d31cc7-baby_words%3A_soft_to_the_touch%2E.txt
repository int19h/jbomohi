pe'i the meaning of soft-like-velvet is that it yields slightly under
slight to moderate force, while saying nothing about its ability to
undergo larger deformations. So I think {ranti tengu} is reasonably
accurate. {pruni tengu} or {randa tengu} may also work, though the gloss
"surrender" suggests that randa1 is supposed to be an agent.

Am 06.07.2012 21:33, schrieb Robin Lee Powell:
> {ranti} seems to be about soft like a ripe fruit, not soft like
> velvet.  What would you use for the latter?  Is {ranti tengu}
> malrarbau?

ranti is about yielding to pressure, not necessarily about the surface. 
You can say:
lo do flira cu ranti lo nu pencu
"Your face is soft when I touch it." (as opposed to firm)

If you want to call the surface "soft", I suggest xutla.

Of course you can mix them depending on your needs.

Yes, velvet isn't really smooth.

There's a few words I found for velvet, but none of them are about the
/texture/ of velvet, which is what it seems you're asking for.

Specifically, demsilka, ranbu'u, and krebu'u.

I would suggest {skapi tengu}, but only if we can specify the "pelt"
meaning of skapi.
