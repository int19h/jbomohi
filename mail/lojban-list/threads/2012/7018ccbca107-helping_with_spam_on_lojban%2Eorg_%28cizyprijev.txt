OK, *completely* new spam procedure, ignore everything I said
before.  Note that this is a list that should be performed in this
order.

1.  Go to http://www.lojban.org/tiki/tiki-lastchanges.php and find
the usernames and IPs causing the trouble.

2.  Use the banning interface at
http://www.lojban.org/tiki/tiki-admin_banning.php to ban the
usernames and, if consistent, the IPs.

3.  If OLD pages were modified, go to the page, click on "History"
at the bottom, and click the "b" button for an appropriate unspammed
version in the Action column to rollback to that version.

4.  If NEW pages were created, email me with the userids; I now have
a script for that purpose.

Speaking from experience with Wikipedia, not specifically the
lojban.org wiki:

Wikipedia is conservative about blocking ip addresses, even
when they have a demonstrated outburst of spam/vandalism.

That community has created a large number of tools to revert
and cleanup problems, and defaults to soft-security.  Including
the sometimes ridiculous of giving a stern warning on the talk
page of an IP address in the process of vandalism.

Wikipedia likely has a greater need for a 'default-open' policy
than lojban.org, but I do think the ban-hammer should be a last
resort, with prevention and development of better cleanup tools
our higher priority.

The tension being of course: how much spam are you willing to
prevent if it turns away a legitimate users?  I mentioned the
Where Are Your Keys? wiki already, and I went to default-closed
there: you gotta e-mail me for an account, and that doesn't affect
the 3 people that are 99% of the edits there.

-Alan
