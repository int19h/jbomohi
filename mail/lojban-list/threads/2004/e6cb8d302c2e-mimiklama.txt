> I thought you could add any number of cmavo to the beginning of any

> the consonant cluster? So for me {mimiklama} = {mi mi klama} (and 
> {mimikla} would be {mi mikla} with an unknown gismu *mikla).
 
Correct.  Spaces are totally optional in Lojban if stress information
is available:  thus mimiklAma is unambiguously "mi mi klama", and
"mimIklama" is unambiguously "mi mikla ma", the nonexistence of "mikla"
being irrelevant; in order to get "mi mI klama" you must say "mi mI. klama".
 
IMHO the Right Thing is to change all whitespace to dots to begin with, and
then work entirely with letters, stress, and pause, which are the terms
in which Nora's pseudocode is written anyway.
 
Just to document what the O.P. does: it checks each whitespace/dot delimited
word as follows:
 
	if it ends in a consonant, CMENE;
	if it contains CC (ignoring all instances of "y" and "'"), BRIVLA;
	if it contains CC (ignoring all instances of "y" and "'"), BRIVLA;
	otherwise, split into cmavo before each consonant.

This handles canonically written Lojban, except that "byfy" will be
misparsed as BRIVLA.  (The rules for when "." may be omitted after BY
are a wart anyhow.)

> And otherwise tosmabru-words wouldn't exist, unless I'm 
> misunderstanding something.
 
Right.

Just to give (geeky) people an idea of how old the O.P. is:

It was originally developed on a PC AT with a 10 MB hard disk and a '286
chip running Microsoft Xenix (a Unix System III variant), with version
5 of the Microsoft C compiler for Xenix, which had a cross-compilation
mode for DOS.  And it was written at a time when I had not yet decided
to adopt function prototypes, because I was concerned about lack of
portability to other Unix systems, whose pcc-based C compilers didn't
yet support them.

On Thursday 18 March 2004 08:04, John Cowan wrote:
> This handles canonically written Lojban, except that "byfy" will be
> misparsed as BRIVLA.  (The rules for when "." may be omitted after BY
> are a wart anyhow.)

Version 5 of the morphology algorithm (which is in the valfendi package) has a 
separate rule for words ending in 'y':

  B.  If the piece ends in 'y':
    I.  Search backward for a consonant.
    II. If you find one:
      a.  If it is preceded by a consonant, resolve the piece as an error.
      b.  If it is not preceded by a consonant, break before the consonant
          and resolve the second piece as a cmavo.
    III.If you do not find one, resolve the piece as a cmavo.

The rule for pauses after BY can be loosened to "A pause is required between a 
word ending in 'y' and a following word containing CCV or CVV." This allows 
{kybu.moi} instead of {ky.bumoi}. Also, since stressed 'Y' never occurs in a 
brivla, the pause can be omitted between stressed BY and a following brivla; 
e.g. {ledYcIlta} means "D's thread" whereas {ledycIlta} means "is a hypha".

Both version 4 and version 5 lex {kyladjan} as {ky la djan} even though it has 
no pauses.

and the -s option is specified, it is assumed to be on the syllable before 
the last, skipping syllables with 'y' in them.

in jbofihe.

The space at the end of a brivla indicates the stress: it is always
on the vowel before the last one.

> I guess what I'm asking is "in absence of stress indicators, does
> tosmabru still happen?".  What about "miklamami"?

You can't have absence of stress indicators. {mi klamami} is 
{mi klamAmi}, where klamami is a fu'ivla. 

> > Currently, what follows doesn't have to be a cluster as long as a
> > cluster follows "soon" after - e.g. {mijimpe} is {mi jimpe} even
> > though the cluster "mp" isn't immediately after "mi".

> Even without the stress?

The way to mark stress is to leave a space after a brivla.

You must always be able to tell where the stress falls, otherwise
you can't be sure you are parsing correctly. Stress is an essential
part of the Lojban morphology.
