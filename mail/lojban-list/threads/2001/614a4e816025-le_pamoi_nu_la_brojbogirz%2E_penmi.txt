On Sun, 25 Feb 2001, Avital Oliver wrote:
>pu nu'i re lo djedi 

{re djedi} is sufficient, but {lo djedi be li re} is the proper way to say it.
After {pu nu'i} they are equivalent, but after {ca'o} (during) they are not.
{ca'o re djedi} means "during two days (which may or may not be adjacent)", but
{ca'o lo djedi be li re} means "during a two-day interval". Without the
termset, {pu re djedi} means "before two days"; some languages (e.g. Greek -
pro duó émeras) actually say this for "two days ago".

>pu nu'i re lo djedi mi penmi loi pendo

I would have said: {za lo djedi be li re mi pu penmi loi pendo}.

I know that the book proposes the use of termsets for this,
but I find it too confusing, I'm not even sure of the grammar
of termsets, so I can't tell if your sentence is correct.

Also {re lo djedi} is not strictly a period of two days duration,
but two separate periods of one day. It would mean that you met
on two occasions.

>.i le girzu fi ze lo prenu .e re lo prenu ba jorne le
>gy.

{le gy} is incorrect, it should be just {gy}.

Also, you have {ze lo prenu e re lo prenu} filling
the x3 place of {jorne}.

You want something like {le girzu cu zemei ije re prenu
ba jorne gy}

>.i puki mi tavla fi le citri nu la lojban.

le citri be la lojban

>.e ba fe mi'a nunsnu fi

{le se smadi be la sapir,uorf}, {ku} not needed.

>je loi mupli be le nu la lojban.
>xamgu mau le glibau

{e} instead of {je}.

{la lojban xamgu mau le glibau} means "Lojban is good
with English as more". You mean something like {la lojban
cu xagmau le glibau}.
