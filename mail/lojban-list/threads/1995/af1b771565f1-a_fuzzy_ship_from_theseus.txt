Fuzzy logic always comes up with regard to {jei}, but I don't
really understand of what use it is. Besides the trivial examples
like:
        le jei ti blanu cu du li pibimu
        The extent of truth of "this is blue" is 0.85

which I suppose nobody would ever want to use, {jei} doesn't seem
to be all that relevant to fuzzy usage. What could be useful would
be a way of assigning some truth scale to what is being said, not
to some quoted sentence. There are (at least) two ways to do
this. One is to use tanru with {mutce}, {traji}, etc. You can't
ask for more fuzziness than tanru. Another way is to use attitudinals,
for instance the scale:

ju'ocai    absolute certainty
ju'osai
ju'o
ju'oru'e   weak certainty
ju'ocu'i   uncertainty
ju'onai    impossibility

Adding that to a sentence is somewhat like giving it a fuzzy truth
value. So I can say {ju'osai ti blanu} "This is ceratinly blue",
or {ju'oru'e ti blanu} "This is blue, I suppose".

But I don't see how {jei}, or {ni}, can be put to use for any
of this.

Jorge
