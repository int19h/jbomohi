=== 2 | 2015-07-17T03:43:57Z | "Spheniscine (la zipcpi)" <spheniscine@gmail.com> | 722032d4-3574-4aba-971a-deb1372c5c9c@googlegroups.com | mail/lojban-list/cur/1437104637.ab3c479b879e9e1f.jbomohi:2,S
Ah yes, the syllabic consonants. I have two solutions to them; one that 
will (hopefully) allow you to speak Lojban as she is defined, and another 
that is non-standard as of now but shouldn't cause many problems:

1. (official solution) Note that only *sonorants* (r, m, n, l) may be 
syllabic. Why? Because sonorants are special in that you can hold a note 
with them. Try it. Other Lojban consonants are called *obstruents* and 
can't have a note held. Try {b} for example.

Thus, practice holding notes with r/m/n/l, then practice sandwiching them 
between two consonants as if they are a vowel, "cidj-rrrr-spageti", and 
hopefully you'll master them.

2. (non-standard solution) Just mentally add a {y} before the syllabic 
consonant, and pronounce it as {cidjyrspageti}. For all practical purposes, 
this cannot be confused by anything else currently accepted in Lojban 
morphology. 

There are theoretical fu'ivla/zi'evla that *would* be confusable, e.g. 
{xazd,m,ru} would become {xazdymru}, which is a lujvo (xazdo zei mruli), 
but none are actually defined or used. There is a proposal in the works to 
ban zi'evla like {xazdmru} altogether, and allow {cidjyrspageti} to be an 
allowed alternate spelling of {cidjrspageti}

----
Additionally, there are often defined alternatives to zi'evla with syllabic 
sonorants. {cidjrspageti}, for instance, is often just {spageti}.

I often pronounce the syllabic 'r' as in English "badgers", but you can also 
roll it. Try saying "Strč prst skrz krk". (Don't actually strčiť prst skrz 
krk. Just say it.) The 'r' is used as a syllable nucleus, and as far as I 
know, it's rolled.

The other syllabic consonants are 'n', 'l', and 'm', of which 'm' is not used 
as an interfix. They should be easier to pronounce.

On Friday, July 17, 2015 03:31:04 Spheniscine wrote:
> I pronounce the second syllable of "badgers" as {djyrz} though.

In my idiolect of English, [ɚ] is phonemically /əɹ/; there's no difference 
between {djyrz} and {djrz} in my English. In my Lojban, however, {yr} is [ər] 
or [əɾ], while vocalic {r} is often [ɚ].

I made up a pair of words that differ by a vocalic {r} versus a schwa {y}: 
{rirxrxrazdani rirxyxrazdani}. (Hrazdan is a river in Armenia.) In that word I 
pronounce {rxrxr} as a simultaneous uvular and alveolar trill with the voice 
turned on and off. In {bakrto} though, the {r} is [ɚ].
