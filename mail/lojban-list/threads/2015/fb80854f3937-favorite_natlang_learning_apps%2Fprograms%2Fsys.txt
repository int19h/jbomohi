I'd recommend Pimsleur which makes many people actually talk. It's an audio
course (I also tried other courses like Glossika and Michele Thomas, they
didn't work for me). You just listen to mp3 files and that's it. No
developers have managed to reproduce this powerful method because people
are lazy these days.

For Memrise I'd recommend any courses that teach actual phrases, not
isolated words. Same for Anki.

Because, you know, it's not Lojban. Verb forms change in Spanish (although
for Lojban recommendations are the same). Yo hablo, tu hablas ...

Also Duolingo.

But once again these Supermemo-based flashcard apps (Pimsleur is not one of
them) are ... idk they mostly don't teach real language, they teach grammar.

That's what you once complained about Lojban that it lacks idiomatics.
Idiomatics not in the sense of metaphors, but ready to use template phrases
(vlapoici'e as opposed to vlapoigunma).

For Lojban it's fine, it's mostly a formal grammar language. For Spanish
it's largely not.

I don't know about programs for learning languages. My favorite method is to 
get a book in the language I'm learning, and the same book in a language I 
already know, and read them together. Of course, this is in addition to 
talking with people who speak the language.

What languages do you already know? Knowing another Romance language will help 
immensely; knowing another Indo-European language also helps, as the six 
person-number forms of verbs are similar among most of them.

Spanish verb tenses are similar to English ones. There's an extra distinction 
in the past tenses, and the present participle comes in adverbial and 
adjectival forms, but progressives, past subjunctives, and conditionals are 
formed similarly, and even "I'm going to <verb>" translates literally into 
Spanish.

As for a learning method, I'd suggest seeing if you can get the AssimI'm 
stuff. Last time I checked the company was still producing these and 
provide a quick way to get the sound of the language and many basic words 
with corresponding grammar into your memory. There is also an online 
program where the basic levels of most language, including Spanish called 
LIVEMOCHA. It also has ways to record sound files so you can hear yourself 
and a setup for written and spoken exchanges with natural speakers. it is 
like Rosetta Stone in many ways, except definitely not in price.
