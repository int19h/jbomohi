I just saw this, and Bob's answer is "I don't know".

I don't think I have ever actually used a lujvo form for this concept, 
and I don't know what others have used.  The CLL text does, however, say 
that the lowest algorithm score is for “lojbaugri” and barring any usage 
history to the contrary, IMHO, the best scoring word should have a 
dictionary entry, even if it only points to some word that is preferred 
in usage.  Someone who knows the algorithm will try to look it up based 
on the scoring.

Back when I was planning the dictionary, I actually wanted up to three 
entries: the best scoring one (for someone who knows the algorithm, but 
wants to check the definition/place structure), the fully-expanded form 
"lojbybangygirzu" (for someone who doesn't know the rafsi) if it is 
different, and any form that is preferred in usage to those two.

I understand that the current concept for jbovlaste would make it hard 
to enforce creating all three entries (though I can imagine a software 
approach that might be used to generate the algorithmic and fully 
expanded forms with cross-reference pointers to the word-form which is 
defined, if they do not already exist for a given word.  This might even 
be a problem with the current jbovlaste, since I suspect that someone 
currently could add a different form of a lujvo with a different 
definition, and that error would not be detected.  (But that isn't a CLL 
production issue).
