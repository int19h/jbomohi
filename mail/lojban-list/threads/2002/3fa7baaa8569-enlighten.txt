Using gusni would be unacceptable metaphor, I think.  It may be the case
that most (maybe all?) cultures use the KNOWING IS SEEING metaphor, but
that won't work in Lojban - you could probably get away with a
metaphorical use of gusni in a text (come to think of it, I've used that
same gismu in that way myself), but lujvo are more problematic.  Lujvo,
like tanru, are metaphorical by definition, but in a rather more
restricted sense of "metaphorical"; i.e. in using "gu'isanji" you are
saying that someone is aware of something in a way that is related to
photons.

Which lujvo you choose to make depends on what sense of the word 
"enlighten" you want. "sajgau" is a possibility - "cause to become aware".

To start with, it wouldn't be transitive like "enlighten".

As a metaphor I would tend to see the x3 of {gusni} as the
person throwing light on an issue, x1 as the clarification
and x2 as the issue to be elucidated. There is no place
for the person to be enlightened, which I suppose is
someone who becomes a source of light and not someone
illuminated. I have used {cikygau lo tergu'i} for lighting
a lamp.

>Are there other possibilities?

Some possibilities:

ciksi - explain
jmigau - make understand
jungau - tell, inform, make know
sajgau - make aware
