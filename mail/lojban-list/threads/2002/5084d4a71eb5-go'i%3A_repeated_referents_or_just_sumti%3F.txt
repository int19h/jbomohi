Does {go'i} repeat the referents of the sumti of the previous the bridi
or just the sumti?  That is, does {le mlatu cu catlu mi. i go'i le
gerku} necessarily refer to the same cats in the second statement or
just another group of {ro le su'o mlatu}?

Is
  le mlatu cu catlu mi. i go'i le gerku
the same as
  le mlatu cu catlu mi. i le mlatu cu catlu le gerku
or
  le mlatu cu catlu mi. e le gerku
?

In the last the same X described as a cat looks at both me and the dog,
while in the middle one the X looking at me is not necessarily the Y
looking at the dog.

Which one is {go'i} equivalent to?

la tanatos cusku di'e

>Does {go'i} repeat the referents of the sumti of the previous the bridi
>or just the sumti?

The referents.

(Strictly speaking, the sumti are the referents, but you
follow the usual tradition we have here of misusing 'sumti'
to refer to the words rather than to the arguments themselves.)

>That is, does {le mlatu cu catlu mi. i go'i le
>gerku} necessarily refer to the same cats in the second statement or
>just another group of {ro le su'o mlatu}?

The same cats. Use {go'ira'o} to update referents.

>Is
>   le mlatu cu catlu mi. i go'i le gerku
>the same as
>   le mlatu cu catlu mi. i le mlatu cu catlu le gerku
>or
>   le mlatu cu catlu mi. e le gerku
>?

The latter. The former as well in most contexts. It would be
very bad manners to use a bare {le mlatu} again for a different
group of cats.
