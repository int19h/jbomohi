We first need to have a set of agreed upon place structures for the 
lujvo.  And so far there has been little work at devising place structures 
for any lujvo, nor for verifying the place structures of the lujvo that 
have been structured.

I haven't looked at xod's effort, but there is a lot more work needed in 
the gismu thesaurus, merely in thesaurusizing the oblique places.

The oblique places was why we abandoned the Roget's scheme, BTW - it really 
doesn't work for multiplace gismu.  You could thesaurusize based on the 
individual places (which are nounish) of each gismu, but Roget's includes 
categories that are nounish, verbish, and adjectivish all mixed together, 
and gismu themselves don't work well as verbs or adjectives without the 
place-limiting use of converters (i.e. the Roget's category for xe klama 
would likely not be the same as for klama).  We really need a new concept 
of how to break down the gismu (or brivla) - either that or risk the 
malropno equation of a gismu to the sum of its individual places.

lojbab

Yes, the thesaurus is mine and I am responsible for all the
incongruities therein. The categorization has been done few years
ago in some haste and certainly needs to be amended.
I am collecting a list of corrections and plan to publish a revised 
version after I finish my Russian translation of gi'uste (in a couple
of months).

Meanwhile, I am open to further criticism. Please send me your
suggestions.

Concerning soya {sobde}, I plead guilty but beg to consider
as a mitigating circumstance the fact that it is explicitely called 
"grain" in the gi'uste:

sobde: x1 is a quantity of soya [grain/bean] of species/strain x2

Isn't it possible to refer to any eatable seed as "grain"?
If not, one can put {sobde} into vegetables category
(I am reluctant to create a special category just for one word).

> Isn't it possible to refer to any eatable seed as "grain"?

Normally, no; no one would refer to walnuts or lima beans as "grains".
Normally, "grain" refers only to the seeds of cereal grasses (as wheat,
rye, maize, barley, etc.), but apparently in commercial/legal usage
(not in ordinary speech) soybeans are reckoned grains as well.
