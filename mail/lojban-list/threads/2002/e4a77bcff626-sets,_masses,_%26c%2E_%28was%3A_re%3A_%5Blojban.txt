My point is that, as Jorge often reminds us, we hardly ever need
to talk about sets in the strict sense, so the lV'i gadri are
wasted. But we do often want to talk about groups/teams (appies
filling a bowl), and, I think, anything that can be described
in terms of sets can also be described in terms of sets/teams
-- all properties that sets have are also properties that groups/
teams have. Hence if we did want to distinguish between a bowl
full of apple and a bowl full of apples, I would suggest making
the first be full of lei apple and the second full of le'i apple.

=== 13 | 2002-03-05T15:50:28Z | Jorge Llambias <jjllambias@hotmail.com> | f241bntu04vwndwmhiu0000fda5@hotmail.com | mail/lojban-list/cur/1015343428.ad6899aa4ad58e67.jbomohi:2,S

la and cusku di'e

>1. How DO you say "apple" (as in "the bowl was full of apple") in
>Lojban, without using inherently vague tanru, and without having
>to define a lujvo for every such mass term?

Since there is no gismu in Lojban meaning "x1 is a quantity
of apple (the substance)", you can't refer to a quantity of
apple substance unless you use a lujvo or a fu'ivla. The
article {loi} does not extract apple material from apples
or shirt material from shirts. In English you wouldn't
say "a bowl full of shirt" if the bowl was full of cloth.
Some words in English work for both the object and for the
main substance of that object, but Lojban is more strict
in that respect. It would seem that {loi} has nothing to
do with mass nouns in English.

like:
