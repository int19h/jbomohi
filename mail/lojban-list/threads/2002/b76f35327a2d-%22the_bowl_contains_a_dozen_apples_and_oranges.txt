le gankabri cu vasru 12 najnimre ja plise. This of course does not imply that 
it contains at least one apple and at least one orange, but that's the way 
one would normally say it. Or {najnimre jonai plise} if you wanted to say 
explicitly that none of them is both an apple and an orange.

A very precise way to say it:

tansi lo paremei be le'i plise jo'e le'i najnimre

Note that a "kabri" however "gan-" is something you consume the contents of
by lifting the container; "tansi" captures the semantics of fruit-bowl
better.

la kreig cusku di'e

>There are many style differences also. For example, I would say it as "le
>gankabri gai najnimre joi plise cu vasru".

That says that each of 12 things, each of which is a
najnimre joi plise, is contained in the bowl.

Pierre's {ja} works but as he says it doesn't guarantee
that they are not all apples or all oranges.

I would say {le gankabri cu vasru lo najnimre joi plise paremei}
which is not very precise but I think is hard to misinterpret.

I'm not sure whether {lo paremei be loi najnimre kujoi loi plise}
is strictly required to contain both. (Of course Griceanly it is
so required.)
