I was confused about the referent of {ri}, when the last sumti has other 
sumti nested within it. The CLL <http://dag.github.io/cll/7/6/> is somewhat 
clear on this point, but Gleki thought the opposite, so here we are.

Example: mi jinvi lo du'u do crebi'o la .lojban. ca lo cabdei

After this sentence, does {ri} point at {la .lojban.} or {lo du'u ... 
cabdei}? And what's the shortest way you would refer to the other one?

I also think it is unclear. Both {lo cabdei} and {lo du'u...} are in a
sense completed "at the same time". In which sense do you think it is
clear? I don't find any examples like to this one.

The wave lessons seem to suggest that it should be {lo cabdei}, but this
information is hidden inside an exercise:

le'e prenu poi ke'a kakne lo nu tavla fo la .lojban. cu tavla *ru* ...
With the comment: ri is not correct, because it refers to la .lojban.
(http://www.lojban.org/tiki/wavelessonscontinued, lesson 15)

> And what's the shortest way you would refer to the other one?

You could use the lerfu anaphora, so cy for {lo cabdei}, and assuming
you're in the next sentence you could go for {lo se go'i} for the
du'u-abstraction. Maybe you could even use dy. And of course, if you're
desperate, use goi, *grin*.

The CLL is indeed rather clear on this point: "Note also that sumti within
other sumti, as in quotations, abstractions, and the like, are counted in
the order of their beginnings; thus a lower level sumti...is considered to
be more recent than a higher level sumti that contains it." So {ri} points
to {lo cabdei} here. Referring to {lo du'u ... cabdei} once would be
easiest with {lo se go'i}.

mi'e la latro'a mu'o
