Actually, I was just wondering b/c I was reading about attitudinals and 
having a hard time imagining there is enough phonological "space" to be 
very clear when speaking, e.g. mishearing .u'o as u'u takes one from 
courage to repentance. That's a big difference in meaning for such a small 
phonological shift. I wonder about this with much of the language, but it 
stands out here in particular.

=== 5 | 2014-08-21T15:18:37Z | ".arpis." <rpglover64+jbobau@gmail.com> | calm==bxlcxak-ukhkvny1o+ztmabhyygq9sbghzcoh_ur=j2nq@mail.gmail.com | mail/lojban-list/cur/1408634317.22bf8470c6260831.jbomohi:2,S
{ba'a nai ju'o ru'e} Robin speaks to his two daughters at least partially
in lojban, and they speak back.

As for clarity of vowels, I try to think of the vowel pronunciations in
*any* language other than English, which I feel has the worst examples for
vowel pronunciation if you're looking to speak lojban. I think Italian,
Spanish, or Japanese vowel pronunciation is the best for me to model after.

One of the other things I know I need to pay attention to (as an English
speaker) is *not* accidentally saying {ou} when I should be saying {o} in
lojban at ends of words.
