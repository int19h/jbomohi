 the short i can often be inserted phonetically (id est, when spoken, but not written) into words that are unusual for english speakers for ease of expression, turning zdani into z(short i sound)dani, or zidani. that helps for those strange consonant clusters, but otherwise doesnt exist in lojban. sadly no short a sound, making those with names that use it, the short i, or the th sound use substitution or make a new name instead ( like i did).

No, he can't.  'z' is a voiced consonant, and 't' is unvoiced.  "ztran." would
have to be changed to "stran.", "zdran.", or "zytran." in order to be an
acceptable Lojban word.  Even if he chose one of the first two, any 'i' sound
between the first two consonants would be nonstandard and would only be used by
those having trouble with the cluster.

Short i can be substituted with "i" or "e"; short a with "e" or "a". "a" is 
avoided in certain positions in names because of the no "la" rule, e.g. 
Lansing can't be "lansin", but can be "lensin".
