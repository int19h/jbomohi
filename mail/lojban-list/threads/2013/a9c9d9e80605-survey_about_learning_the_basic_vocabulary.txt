Hi!
I want to start a little survey about how people
learn the basic vocabulary of Lojban. I do this just out
of curiosity, that’s all.

If you want to participate, just reply to this e-mail and
answer these questions:
1) Did you learn any Lojban vocabulary so far?
If you said “no”, the survey is done for you. If you said “yes”, go on:
2) How many of the gismu do you have memorized right now?
3) How many rafsi?
4) And how many cmavo?
5) When did you start learning vocabulary?
6) How often (roughly) do (or did) you learn?
7) On a regular basis?
8) Which learning tool or learning method do you use?
Extra question for those who already memorized all gismu, rafsi and cmavo:
9) When did you “make it”? (a rough estimate)

Notes: I only talk about the _official_ gismu, cmavo and rafsi.
Knowledge of other valsi or rafsi is not relevant to this survey.
With “memorized” I mean something like: I throw you out of bed and ask you
“Quick! What’s the definition of ‘jalge’?” then you automatically,
while still not really awake, can say the correct answer.

The moment I heard the first word of Lojban. (no flashcards, just exposure)

I don't sit down and learn words in isolation. I use the language and

life, and I consider that the best practice one can get.

There was a short period in which I used memrise, but on the whole it's 
not my preferred way of learning vocabulary. I learned a lot by 
conversing on IRC and by translating massive amounts of text into 
Lojban. I would also recommend *reading* texts to everyone. It's so much 
more effective than flashcards in my opinion. But to each their own.

On Sunday, November 10, 2013 06:18:23 Wuzzy wrote:
> Notes: I only talk about the _official_ gismu, cmavo and rafsi.

I don't think that's a good criterion for knowing a word. It is one aspect of 
knowing a word, but I know lots of words that I can't instantly define when 
suddenly asked.

If you know the word "bakni", you should be able to point at one when passing 
by it; recognize that "lo nakni bakni cu bunre xunre" makes more sense than 
"lo baktu bakni cu cmaci smuci"; answer correctly "lo bakni ji lo ractu cu se 
sufti?"; and be able to put "bakni", "xarju", and "mirli" correctly in "lo 
_____ cu zmadu lo _____ lo ni ckini lo _____".

Knowing a word by its definition in another language can lead to errors. 
Yesterday I was in church and we were discussing the two goats of Yom Kippur. 
First I said "de los dos chivos, mata uno por azar", which is correct but not 
the usual way of saying it. Later I said "elige un cabrío por lote", which is 
wrong. I picked the wrong word for "lot". (lote=tumspi; suerte=azar=cunso.) 
Similarly one can know that "jdari" means "hard", and "nandu" means "hard", 
and pick the wrong one.
