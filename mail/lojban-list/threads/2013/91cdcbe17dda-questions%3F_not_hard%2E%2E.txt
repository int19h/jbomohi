Morphological plurals are NOT optional in Lojban; they are not even (strictly speaking) possible.  You could make plural predicates (presumably pluralizing the first argument) by adding  {zma, mau} maybe -- or the combinative form of some numerical operator.  But why, when syntactic plurals (when needed at all) are so easy (any of them).  The other list is too odd to comment on, except to ask what would count as not synonyms if they are the common or gloss words.  "he/she/it" are English specific, and "our" probably is too; "because/for/be/has" are some ambiguous/vague as to be useless as guides to what you want and the others are just gismu.

There is no grammatical number at all in Lojban.

> 2- what is the gloss word or the most common word -but not synonyms- for
> each of
> 
> he:             she:
> it:              our:                 

There is no gender in Lojban. "he", "she", and "it" are all expressed the same 
way, which could be "ko'a", "fo'a", any of the other 8 in the series, "ri", 
"ra", "ru", "ti", "ta", "tu", or a lervla. "our" could be "mi'o", "mi'a", or 
"ma'a", depending on clusivity; it is placed between the article and the 
selbri, or in a pe-phrase afterward.

> up:             down:       right
> -direction-:              left  -direction-:

ga'u, ni'a, ri'u, zu'a

> because:                  for:             be:      has:

"because" can be "ri'a", "seja'e", "ni'i", or "ki'u". "has" can be "ponse" or 
"tolcau", or can be translated by various brivla depending on what is had. 
"be" is "du", but the English word has other uses. "for" needs context to be 
translated.

=== 8 | 2013-04-22T21:56:22Z | Ian Johnson <blindbravado@gmail.com> | ca+gztgla=geabphl1qi=j9odu1xbsrb_jj_o=pcgntw_uvb7vw@mail.gmail.com | mail/lojban-list/cur/1366667782.3b056531ec22141a.jbomohi:2,S
The solution depends a little bit on what you want. {za'u lo broda} is one
solution, which has distributivity properties; {lo za'u broda} is another,
which doesn't have distributivity but is vague about whether more than one
of them actually does whatever it is you're saying. {lo broda za'u mei} is
another option, which is essentially the same as {loi za'u broda} (which
does say that more than one of them are actually involved).

mi'e la latro'a mu'o
