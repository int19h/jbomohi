> If you said “no”, the survey is done for you. If you said “yes”, go on:
> 2) How many of the gismu do you have memorized right now?

Right now, probably around 1/2.  When I was actively using the language. 
  I was able to do the full gismu list in recall (knowing the Lojban 
word from the English keyword) or recognition (being able to give 
English keyword for the Lojban) in excess of 97% accuracy as measured by 
LogFlash.   At that level, typing accuracy at speed is more important 
than actually knowing the words - most of my errors were typos.

(Recall is substantially harder than recog - I was essentially perfect 
on recog long before I mastered recall)

> 3) How many rafsi?

Probably around 30% on recog or recall.  I was at one point up to 80% on 
recall, and near 100% on recog.

> 4) And how many cmavo?

Probably around 20% on recog and 30% on recall.  I was at one point up 
to 80% on recall and near 100% on recog.

> 5) When did you start learning vocabulary?

1987 %^)

> 6) How often (roughly) do (or did) you learn?

When I actively did LogFlash, it was on a daily basis, an hour a day 
when first learning, and 20-30 minutes a day for maintenance.  I stopped 
doing active maintenance before 1991, but was able to get 50% recall on 
first pass, and above 80% recall on a second pass through all of the 
words, and in excess of 90% after that (50-100 words a day).  I did this 
several times during the 1990s after gaps of 6 months or more.  I have 
not significantly used LogFlash since 2003 or 2004 when we last held 
LogFest annually at our house in Fairfax (it was part of our annual 
preparation so spend at least some time).

> 7) On a regular basis?

When learning, compulsively regular.  Maintenance, I have been intermittent.

> 8) Which learning tool or learning method do you use?

LogFlash, flashcard program, which is a DOS program that still runs up 
to WinXP, and under DOSBOX with Windows 7.  It is still available on the 
Lojban website.

The program implemented an algorithm that JCB developed for Loglan back 
in the 1960s-1970s and extensively tested with paper flash cards. It was 
fine-tuned for optimized vocabulary learning and was awesomely 
effective.  Any flash card method will probably work to some extent, but 
it must go both directions (recog and recall) to be able to fully use 
the vocabulary.

We also used Nora's random sentence generator and random lujvo generator 
to learn and test vocabulary in context.  But that did not provide any 
measurement of how skilled we were (we were trying to link these 
programs into LogFlash , but no one except us ever reported using the 
rafsi version of the program, and Nora stopped actively working on it in 
the 1990s).

> Extra question for those who already memorized all gismu, rafsi and cmavo:
> 9) When did you “make it”? (a rough estimate)

1989 for gismu.  1991 for cmavo, 1992 for rafsi (but we remade several 
rafsi in 1994, and I never went through LogFlash with the remade list).
