count in Lojban is:

pa .i re .i ci .i vo .i mu ...

If you don't insert the ".i" between numbers, you end up saying one
large number instead of counting.

http://dag.github.com/cll/18/12/

The ability to utter bare numbers as grammatical Lojban sentences is
primarily intended for giving answers to “xo” questions. (Another use,
obviously, is for counting off physical objects one by one.)

This implies {pa .i re .i ci}, not {pa re ci} for counting, right?

Because honestly, I've used both when counting to myself (stitches, during
knitting and crocheting), depending on whether the extra syllable was
convenient.

Similarly, I can imagine counting time in music with {pa re ci} when
counting quarter notes and {pa .i re .i ci .i vo .i} when counting eighths.
I'm not sure what horrible mangling of grammar I would do for 16th notes.

2011/2/13 Jorge Llambías <jjllambias@gmail.com>

I'm not entirely sure that Lindar was talking about counting as in "1, 2, 3,
4" in the first place.

count–verb (used with object)
1. to check over (the separate units or groups of a collection) one by one
to determine the <http://dictionary.reference.com/browse/the> total number;
add up; enumerate: He counted his tickets and found he had ten.
2. to reckon up; calculate; compute.
3. to list or name <http://dictionary.reference.com/browse/name> the
numerals up to: Close your eyes and count ten.
4. to include in a reckoning; take into account: There are five of us here,
counting me.
5. to reckon to the credit of another; ascribe; impute.
6. to consider or regard: He counted himself lucky to have survived the
crash.

Like most English words, there are multiple senses to "count". "1,2,3,4"
would be definition 1 a/o 2, "1.4653*10^4" would, I think, be 2.

To quote Lindar:
"To expand on the question, I mean rather than saying 14326 as "one four
three two six" instead of "fourteen thousand three hundred twenty-six" are
there any other counting methods? Counting exponentially, or any other
system that isn't just saying all of the numbers starting at the highest
place and ending at the singles?"

I'm pretty sure by "counting exponentially", he is referring to scientific
notation (2.25*10^2), or possibly factorization (225 = 3^2*5^2).
