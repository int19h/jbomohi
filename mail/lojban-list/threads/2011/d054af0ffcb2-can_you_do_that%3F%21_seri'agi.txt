I was just perusing the lojban wiki page and found this example:
*seri'agi mi jgari lei djacu gi mi jgari le kabri*
With-physical-effect I grasp the-mass-of water, I grasp the cup.
I grasp water, since I grasp the cup.

I didn't know you could just do: TAG+gi broda gi brode.  That's awesome.
 Although, I have to admit, I really am not at all sure about what is going
on here.

So is {baugi la lojban gi mi tavla} the same thing as {mi tavla bau la
lojban}?  But wait... it's like the second {gi} is linking two bridi, so
would it have to be {baugi jbobau gi mi tavla}?

So is {mi nelci do mu'i lo nu do nelci mi} = {mu'igi do nelci mi gi mi nelci
do}?

Also, I'm suspicious about whether the original author wanted {seri'agi} or
{ri'agi}.

http://jbotcan.org/cllc/c9/s8.html is the relevant CLL section. As I
understand it, you can TAG gi ... gi or JOI gi ... gi any construct
you could use within a GA ... gi construct - so basically, you could
pair sumti, or selbri, or bridi in this way. In some ways, you can
think of it as a forethought version of {.i TAG bo}.

Right from the CLL:

mi mu'i gi viska le cukta gi lebna le cukta

I, motivated by seeing the book, took the book.

Here, we're separating two bridi-tails.

This can be shortened, however, to:

mi mu'i gi viska gi lebna vau le cukta

Elegant, isn't it? I just learned about this yesterday as well.

An example that cropped up on IRC, from vensa:

va'o gi mi troci lo nu kakne lo vajni poi cinri mi gi mi gleki

If you are starting with a tag and would like to comfortably be able
to return to base-level without {cu}, this can act like that. TAG gi
... state a complex bridi which might require obscene amounts of
terminators, gi, and then your main bridi - no {cu} required!

mi'e .kribacr. mu'o
