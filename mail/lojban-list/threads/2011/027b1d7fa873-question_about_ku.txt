That's a weird one.  camxes reports:

  baku mi citka
  text
     sentence
     |- terms
     |  |- term1
     |  |  |- CMAVO
     |  |  |     PU: ba
     |  |  |- CMAVO
     |  |        KU: ku
     |  |- CMAVO
     |        KOhA: mi
     |- BRIVLA
           gismu: citka

And looking at the grammar:

  term-1 <- sumti
          / ( !gek
              ( tag   / FA-clause  free* )
              ( sumti / KU-clause? free* ) )
          / termset
          / NA-clause
            KU-clause
            free*

The second ordered choice is the relevant production: 

  ( !gek
    ( tag   / FA-clause  free* )
    ( sumti / KU-clause? free* ) )

Where the tag rule is eventually matching "ba" and the KU-clause
rule is matching ku.  I can't tell what that KU-clause is doing
there, it's in the EBNF too.

As to why 'ku', I couldn't say (running out of good terminals?), but as to why 
not 'ku' in 'mi ba citka lo plise', that's because it is where it belongs (in 
Lojban, not in Logic, but that is another whole story)(cf 'na').  When it moves 
away , it needs a mark to show that it is not now functioning as a preposition 
or whatever, but in its home function, though not at home.  It may be, in fact, 
as suggested, that the 'ku' is there marks the end of a null term, to indicate 
that this is not being used with a term at all.  (Weird, but then 'ba' does need 
a 'ku' -- I think -- when in prenex, its logical home.)
