I've been thinking about writing the stress with an accent instead of a 
capital letter. The accent need be written only on cmevla. I suggest these 
rules, which aren't complete:

*Syllabic consonants are ignored for counting.
*:míklson
*The stress is by default placed on a certain syllable, counted as if 
diphthongs were pronounced as one syllable even if written otherwise, and on a 
certain vowel in that syllable.
*A syllable is counted if it contains a vowel other than "y". A syllable which 
contains "y" and no other vowel is skipped under conditions which aren't 
exactly clear yet.
*:sautuesrydyfytyn: not clear which syllable gets the stress
*:lylun: the "u" is by default stressed
*:málybar
*:uiliýmben
*A string of vowels is broken into syllables starting from the right.

The stressed vowel of a diphthong is like this:
áu, ái, éi, ói,
uá, ué, uí, uó, uú,
iá, ié, ií, ió, iú,
iý, uý.
The stressed vowel of a triphthong, if there is any, is the middle vowel.

This is loosely based on the system used in Spanish, which I don't fully know. 
There are (AFAIK) four third-person singular past tense forms of verbs 
consisting of one consonant followed by "io": dio, fio, rio, vio. Of these, 
"dio" and "vio" are one syllable and "fio" and "rio" are two, accented on "o" 
(this takes some explanation, particularly for "rio", and is irrelevant). The 
first-person singular present forms of these verbs are "fío" and "río" (the 
latter is also a noun meaning "river"). Since the default stressed vowel of 
"io" is "o", it has to be written on "fío" and "río".

Pierre

I would be extremely in favor of this!

I have personally always found a little bit disturbing the use of
capital letters for marking the stressed syllable.
I suspect there is some historical reason for that (I guess US
typewriters had no accented letters at the time Lojban born :) ).

Nowadays I think we could make good use of accents. Just compare
{jòsefin} with {JOsefin} or {filìp} with {filIP}. Our language is
already ugly enough without having to mix lower and upper case.

I would not care for which accent to use. I would go for the grave
accent (àèìòù) because that's what Italian uses most but I don't feel
so strong about it. Up to the point I won't even be prescriptive on
this: any accent would do.

Hope you get enough people backing up your proposal.

We don't have a clear and general rule on where the default stress is. The 
rule is clear in the case where the last two syllables are O-O, O-OI, OI-O, 
OI-OI, Y-O, or Y-OI, where Y is "y", O is any other single vowel, and OI is 
any falling diphthong not including "y". It is not clear what the rule is if 
the last two syllables are Y-Y, IO-Y, IY-A, or various other combinations 
that don't occur in brivla.

That's true, but it doesn't matter, because "non-standard" or
"non-default" stress is irrelevant.

The important distinction is between obligatory and optional stress.
Obligatory stress must be marked, optional stress is optional, and it
need not be marked.

The only obligatory stress is the stress on the penultimate syllable
of each brivla. That stress is usually marked by a space at the end of
the brivla, which allows you to figure out which syllable is the
penultimate one. If you don't write a space there, then you need to
mark the obligatory stress in some other way, so "denpa bu" or
"dénpabu" or "dEnpabu", or "DENpabu" but not "denpabu" which is a
brivla stressed on the "a".

Optional stress is the stress that is allowed on any syllable of a
cmavo or of a cmevla (you can even stress more than one syllable in a
long cmevla if you wish, but brivla, no matter how long, have only one
stressed syllable). These optional cmavo and cmevla stresses need
never be marked, they don't change the word, "có'o" or "co'ó" is the
same word, just as using different tones or using different variants
of "r" doesn't change the word, but if for some reason you want to
mark it, it doesn't hurt either.
