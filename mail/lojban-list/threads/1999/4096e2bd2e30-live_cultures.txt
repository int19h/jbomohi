Note use of {ru'a}.  It's a speculation/postulation, not an assumption.  I
think languages start out very metaphorical, then some of the metaphor gets
subsumed into the grammar, so the target domain loses any cognitive
connection with the source domain. {mu'a} the Turkish equative suffix
-dir/dIr/dur/dür comes from the old Turkish stem turu- (or was it türü- ?)
meaning "stand", and the progressive suffix -yor- comes from yoru- (modern
Turkish yürü-) meaning "walk", but there's no real connection now.  English
seems to have done this less than many languages, possibly because of it's
Creole origins and isolating structure.  Whorf contrasted Hopi with English
along these lines.
