The following list is a proposal for Lojban chemical element
nomenclature.  The international chemical symbol is given with a
corresponding Lojban name and either a gismu (where one exists)
or a lea'vla built on the name plus the gismu "xukmi", chemical.
English names are given only where needed for clarity. 

An earlier version of this list, prepared by lojbab., appeared in UL2.
ki'e.  This list differs from his partly because the language has changed,
and also because I have placed more emphasis on abstracting an international
pronunciation and less on preserving the symbol-to-name relationship.

Notes:

1) The Lojbanizing of names is normally based on pronunciation, but the
international names of chemical elements do not have internationally
agreed-on pronunciations.  Therefore, the following systematic changes have
been made:

	international "c" is changed to "k", except in "ci" and "ce";
	international final "e" is dropped;
	international "ch", "ph", "rh", "th" are changed to "k", "p", "r", "t";
	international "h" is changed to "x";
	international "la" is changed to "ly" in the name only;
	international "w" is changed to "v" initially and "u" otherwise;
	international "x" is changed to "ks" except initially;
	international "y" is changed to "i";
	Lojban "'" has been inserted as needed into international diphthongs.

2) Einsteinium's name is based on that of Einstein, which Lojbanizes as
.ainstain.

3) Molybdenum contains an impermissible medial "bd", into which I have inserted
a "y" hyphen. 

4) Strontium begins with a consonant triple, which is legal in names;
however, I have inserted a hyphen here also to ease pronunciation.

5) The name-to-lea'vla algorithm is as follows:
	Change any "ly" back to "la".
	Change any other "y" hyphen to "i".
	If the name ends in "-ium", remove the "-um" leaving "-i";
		otherwise, add "-i".
	Then attach a prefix signifying "chemical":
		"xukrn-" if the name begins with a vowel;
		"xukn-" if the name begins with "r";
		"xukr-" otherwise.

6) Remember that in Lojban the ending "-ium" is pronounced as a single
syllable, roughly "yoom".

	Al  Aluminum	Common and important construction material

	P   Phosphorus	Important human nutrient

There is undoubtedly a great deal of glico bias (and even merko bias) in
the gismu list.  It's the product of raw empiricism, nothing more.
However, "metaphor" is the very basis of including a word as a gismu rather
than allowing it to remain a le'avla.  Borrowings do not enter into lujvo,
but otherwise they are full-fledged Lojban brivla.  In general, a word
should be a gismu if good and useful tanru can be made from it.

Naturally, I can't present a list such as Ivan requires.  But then, after all,
some of the concepts on the gismu list barely exist in the source languages.
There is no commonly used Chinese word for "brown", but Lojban has a word
(the Chinese component comes from the dictionary, but it's rare in either
speech or writing).
