What are good ways to replace the English plural noun form? As I
understand, one should generally avoid lV'i, as one generally doesn't
want to talk about the set of X *as a set*, but that lVi can be good?
What about {su'o}, used alone? (It parses.) Other ways?

If I'm not completely mistaken, it would be preferable to use lVi in
situations where the group of things as a group, collectively, have
some property or take some action, and {su'o} in situations where they
individually do so?

You can do it with {su'o}:
{lo su'o re dacti}
means "at least two things", which is pretty much the
same as "things". You need {loi} or {lei} only when the
at least two things act as a mass -- a couple of apples
dropping to the ground at the same time at the same
place counts as a mass, for example.
{loi su'o re plise cu farlu}
Although in most of the cases the lack of this {su'o re}
doesn't lead to ambiguity.

({su'o} on its own means the same as {su'o pa}, btw.)

=== 6 | 2006-06-27T20:35:57Z | John E Clifford <lojban-out@lojban.org> | 20060627203557.86802.qmail@web81315.mail.mud.yahoo.com | mail/lojban-list/cur/1151440557.cec7d7d8ba7fc3b0.jbomohi:2,S
Actually, the "problem" expression is the singular.  Lojban descriptions are of a number of
things; that that number is 1 is as special a case as that it is 83.  So -- context permitting --
the best plural is just the form without internal quantifiers at all.  Now context -- including
external knowledge -- can modify that, and the various gadri have different implications: {lVi}
and {lV'i} are very strongly plural in all contexts, {la} usually suggests singular, {le} less
strongly so, {lo} not at all.
And, of course, whether the apples all falling at the same time is about a "mass" depends upon
what you say: In "The apples fell simultaneously" yes, in "The apples fell at 10:47 a.m. CDT on
June 27, 2006" probably not.
