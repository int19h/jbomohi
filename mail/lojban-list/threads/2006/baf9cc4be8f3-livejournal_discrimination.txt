ta'onai it's not the end of the world.ui. The CLL supports explicit stress,
and even has examples of it.



chapter: The Hill Are Alive With the Sound Of Lojban.
section: Syllabication and stress

The following are Lojban words with stress explicitly shown:

9.6)  dikyjvo
    DI,ky,jvo

(In a fully-buffered dialect, the pronunciation would be: [Èdi k« jI vo].)
Note that the syllable ``ky'' is not counted in determining stress. The
vowel ``y'' is never stressed in a normal Lojban context.

9.7)  .armstrong.
    .ARM,strong.

This is a Lojbanized version of the name ``Armstrong''. The final ``g'' must
be explicitly pronounced. With full buffering, the name would be pronounced:

9.8)  [È/a rI mI sI tI ro nI gI/]

However, there is no need to insert a buffer in every possible place just
because it is inserted in one place: partial buffering is also acceptable.
In every case, however, the stress remains in the same place: on the first
syllable.

The English pronunciation of ``Armstrong'', as spelled in English, is not
correct by Lojban standards; the letters ``ng'' in English represent a velar
nasal (IPA [N]) which is a single consonant. In Lojban, ``ng'' represents
two separate consonants that must both be pronounced; you may not use [N] to
pronounce Lojban ``ng'', although [Ng] is acceptable. English speakers are
likely to have to pronounce the ending with a buffer, as one of the
following:

9.9)  [È/arm stron gI/]
    or [È/arm stroN gI/]
    or even [È/arm stro nIg/]

The normal English pronunciation of the name ``Armstrong'' could be
Lojbanized as:

9.10)    .ARMstron.

since Lojban ``n'' is allowed to be pronounced as the velar nasal [N].

Here is another example showing the use of ``y'':

9.11)    bisydja
    BI,sy,dja
    BI,syd,ja

This word is a compound word, or lujvo, built from the two affixes ``bis''
and ``dja''. When they are joined, an impermissible consonant pair results:
``sd''. In accordance with the algorithm for making lujvo, explained in Chapter
4 <http://www.lojban.org/publications/reference_grammar/chapter4.html>, a
``y'' is inserted to separate the impermissible consonant pair; the ``y'' is
not counted as a syllable for purposes of stress determination.

9.12)    da'udja
    da'UD,ja
    da'U,dja

These two syllabications sound the same to a Lojban listener --- the
association of unbuffered consonants in syllables is of no import in
recognizing the word.

9.13)    e'u bridi
    e'u BRI,di
    E'u BRI,di
    e'U.BRI,di

In Example 9.13 <http://www.lojban.org/tiki/chapter3.html#e9d13>, ``e'u'' is
a cmavo and ``bridi'' is a brivla. Either of the first two pronunciations is
permitted: no primary stress on either syllable of ``e'u'', or primary
stress on the first syllable. The third pronunciation, which places primary
stress on the second syllable of the cmavo, requires that --- since the
following word is a brivla --- the two words must be separated by a pause.
Consider the following two cases:

9.14)    le re nobli prenu
    le re NObli PREnu

9.15) le re no bliprenu
    le re no bliPREnu

If the cmavo ``no'' in Example
9.15<http://www.lojban.org/tiki/chapter3.html#e9d15>were to be
stressed, the phrase would sound exactly like the given
pronunciation of Example 9.14<http://www.lojban.org/tiki/chapter3.html#e9d14>,
which is unacceptable in Lojban: a single pronunciation cannot represent
both.

  John W. Cowan capitalizes the constonants,  however he also states  that
partial buffering is acceptable.

Spacelessness seems to only be explicitly supported by cmAvo groupings.
chapter: The Shape Of Words To Come: Lojban Morphology
section: cmavo

Compound cmavo are sequences of cmavo attached together to form a single
written word. A compound cmavo is always identical in meaning and in
grammatical use to the separated sequence of simple cmavo from which it is
composed. These words are written in compound form merely to save visual
space, and to ease the reader's burden in identifying when the component
cmavo are acting together.

so I guess Alex Martini, you can definatly add spaces between the brIvla and
cmAvo groupings. I find it's easier to see a lOjban vAlsi among English

Lojban does not permit that capitalization on words which obey the
stress rules.

Your post is not valid Lojban. You have not shown that it is. The
Lojban blog appears on the homepage and is the public face of the
language community. It is acceptable to make mistakes in Lojban on the
blog and discuss how to correct them. It is even acceptable to
deliberately show variation that deviates from Lojban, as you have
done. But it may only be done in order to have a thought experiment
for us to discuss. You have to label it as not Lojban. You are not
allowed to pass it off as real Lojban and confuse beginners who come
to the blog.

it. Only you, as the post's author, can edit it without deleting it.
In order for beginners to not be confused by your post, please add
text describing what you do as an illegal variation, something which
is not Lojban but something to think about and discuss on the blog as
a fun and harmless thought experiment for our community. Then we'll
leave it. If you don't, we will have to delete your post.

My understanding is that a space in lojban writing indicates an optional pause. That is, one must pause at a {.}, one may pause at a { }, and one must not pause at any other place. Therefore, a lojban passage written down with no spaces and only showing stress may be the most accurate way to describe that sentence as uttered in one instance, but it might be inaccurate with regard to another instance.

A few general comments - not directed at anyone in particular. First,  
let's not make a major rift over this. I offer my apologies to any I  
have offended. I do, however, stand by most of what I have said.

epkat is correct; I actually can't edit the post. I just assumed  
since I saw the 'pencil' icon that I use to edit my own posts, it did  
the same thing. It lets me look at edit view, but not make changes.

The issues of majuscules and no spaces two different things. I can  
read text where every stressed syllable is indicated, albeit with  
some difficulty. I can't read text with no spaces unless I copy it  
and then go back and put in spaces. Consequently, I don't mind the  
former, but do the latter. And it's the latter which bothered me in  
the lj.

Andreii -- I love your enthusiasm and artwork; I just want to  
*understand* what you are saying. And I can't do that with the  
current form. I want to see your ideas as you see them, especially  
your drawing since I have little artistic talent of my own.

As for the lj entry; I made those comments because of the higher  
publicity of the journal. I feel that it is not the place to use non- 
standard lojban. That kind of thing belongs better on the mailing  
list. I would enjoy to see a long thread debating the merits of the  
orthographies as much as the huge thread on 'all' a while back. But  
until it's accepted by the community, I don't think it belongs on the  
lj. In my mind, the lj is a place where people can see things that  
are examples of good lojban.

I stand with epkat here -- experimental lojban is ok, if and only if  
it is marked as such. And for now, no-spaces orthography is still an  
experimental style.

I would love to see you keep your interest and continue posting to  
the lj. I don't know enough gismu yet to post anything of interest (I  
know more cmavo than gismu, probably) and don't have the time to  
spend learning more. And artwork on the lj is *always* a plus.

To boil this down, I don't personally like the no spaces style even  
though it is grammatical. It might have some use in taking down what  
was said in lojban when making transcripts etc, but at present it's  
not the normal way to write lojban. So I don't feel it's appropriate  
to put it in a place like the lj, unless it's marked as being a

that creativity is aimed at challenging the as-yet 
unestablished-by-habit norms of usage, then it serves to interfere with 
that establishment of norms.

Natural languages have built in standards in the form of a native 
speaking population that learned the language as a mother tongue.

Loglan under JCB erred in being a pursuit of perfection.  Lojban since I
