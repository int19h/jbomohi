On 9/27/07, Marjorie Scherf <mls1@rice.edu> wrote:
>
>  A discussion was started on the beginners list that seemed to fit better
> here. The question is whether something can be a Lojbanic way of talking
> about something if it uses non-Lojban letters. For example, saying {wybu} to
> talk about 'w'. It has been brought up that when talking to beginners,
> discussions of letters are usually done in English. But it would seem to me
> that if we're not worrying about Lojban names for things or Lojban ways to
> name them that it is pointless to use {bu} at all in these beginners'
> discussions. Things like {wybu} or its analogs {qybu} or {hybu} seem like
> inconsistent hybrids. But then, I've only been learning for a few months,
> and it is possible that there are years of precedents for this kind of thing
> that I missed.
>

They don't parse. Non-lojbanic characters can only be expressed inside
{la'o} and {zoi} quotes. Otherwise you have to use circumlocutions such as
{y'y. bu} and those involving {lau} {zai} {tei}.

.i ebu cusku lu .i sepli mi'o le panoxa cibjmagutci fa la ke'avro .ije ma'a
> ponse lo culno me la betsis. me'u .e lo xadba culno tanxe be lo zgikrkazu'u
> .ije manku .ije mi'o dasni lo solri le'otci li'u .i jy. cusku lu ko .e mi
> klama li'u
>
u'i i mi stidi lu le cibjmagutci be li pa no xa li'u i mu'o mi'e
