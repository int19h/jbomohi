> person in the group like that. Say he's blonde. Is there a way I can say

{pa pelxre} == {pa da poi pelkre} => "one X which-is golden-haired"
{pa lo prenu poi pelxre} => "A singular person which-is blonde"
{lo pelxre prenu} => "One or more blonde type-of person"
{ko'a goi (any of the above)} => "It-1, which-is what we will call {any of
the above)"

I'm sure there's other ways to do it as well, but those are the most
obvious, to me at any rate.

=== 3 | 2013-01-03T15:21:46Z | Ian Johnson <blindbravado@gmail.com> | ca+gztgjkxdmtd_ws_u8g1mmlvfa-dvqp2fgj9wt82xh-fp0hnw@mail.gmail.com | mail/lojban-beginners/cur/1357226506.7cf3b240d3169755.jbomohi:2,S
The first version is wrong; that says "there exists exactly one yellow
haired thing which ..." (Rather, there is a very slightly less
controversial version that I tend to subscribe to: "there is exactly one
yellow-haired thing that is at all possible to come up in discussion which
..." The point is that the default universe of discourse should be the *
universe* of discourse, there shouldn't be anything that be reasonably
anticipated to come up in conversation that isn't actually in it.)

If you use su'o instead it's not terrible, but really {da} constructs when
you don't want nice sharp precision tend to be undesirable, in my
experience.

At any rate, here I'd probably say {lo se ke pelxu kerfa}. {le} might also
be appropriate in this context, but I doubt I'd use it. That said, I think
more likely the structure of the discussion would be different among fluent
in-real-life Lojban speakers. (Few of these exist; I, for example, am
pretty quick on IRC, but am almost completely helpless when it comes to
spatial things.) I'd expect a lot of up-front establishment of pronouns,
for example.

mi'e la latro'a mu'o

> Isn't this a situation for {le}, as in {le se pelkre}? As I understand it,

The use of {le} signifies that the user is talking about a specific thing
or things, which may or not be what the speaker is using to describe them.

For example, {le mustlei} "The weasel(s)", could be used to refer to the
employees of a law firm, since, at least in English, lawyers are often
called weasels.

So this is a situation in which you could use {le}, but you don't have to,
as lo pelxre is assumed to refer to the most obvious thing it can be in the
given context
