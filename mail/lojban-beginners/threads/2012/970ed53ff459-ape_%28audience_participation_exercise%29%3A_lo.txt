So, I know I said I was going to do one of these a week, and I'm sorry I
haven't. I haven't been able to think of any good ones until literally just
now.

That said, here's the latest APE:

Come up with words of wisdom for Lojban. There's a specific word for this,
somewhat related to clichè, idiom, psalm, koan, etc., but I can't remember
what it is. Whoever tells me first gets bonus points. :)

You can make something up, you can translate something from another
language, whatever. The only requirement is that it be one or two jufra of
wisdom an old jbopre would believably impart to grandkids.

To start things off:

{lonu lo srera na ve cilre cu se jalge lonu rapli li ci'i}
"A mistake not learned from is repeated endlessly."

That's correct, but in your original sentence, {lo ponse be lo mruli po'o}
means "one who has something that is only a hammer" rather than "one who
has only a hammer", which is reflected in the Lojban be the presence, or
absence in this case, of {ku}. The difference arises from the fact that
po'o is attaching to the selbri of the description sumti instead of the
actual sumti itself.

In other words, {.i lo nanmu po'o cu kakne} and {.i lo nanmu ku po'o kakne}
have (very) different meanings. In the former, "Things that are only men
are able", whereas in the latter "Only the men are able." In the former,
it's the selbri {nanmu} that is the only relevant selbri, whereas in the
latter, it's the sumti {lo nanmu} that is the only relevant sumti.

.i mi'e la tsani mu'o

First, the thing she intended to say, "five cents or a dime", would make the 
right bridi not tautologically true if the conjunction were "jo"; the humor is 
in using a conjunction that makes sense when the amounts are different with two 
amounts that are equal. Second, the right bridi is on the right side of "iju", 
so its truth value is irrelevant. I add "kau" because the statement as a whole 
is not a question.
