And now to see if I understand:

ca - present; when used like this, it means that the bridi is occurring at
the same time as the sumti to the right of ca
lo - convert a selbri to a sumti
nu - event abstractor - nu .. kei is the event
ca'o - continuative; the selbri had already started and hadn't yet finished
la meris - Mary
katna - x1 cuts x2 ... ; can be used to mean chop
lo mudri - wood [of some tree species]

the entire {ca lo nu ... kei} becomes a time tag
la djan - John
co'a - marks krixa as starting
krixa - x1 screams/emits sound x2

did I get most of that?

also, it seems like it would be invalid to use krixa to scream information.
{mi krixa zoi .gy. Get out of bed! .gy.} is clearly wrong, but what about
{mi krixa se du'u zoi .gy. Get out of bed! .gy.}?  Also wrong, right?

All correct.

> also, it seems like it would be invalid to use krixa to scream information.
> {mi krixa zoi .gy. Get out of bed! .gy.} is clearly wrong,

I wouldn't say "clearly wrong". It's just that all that matters is
that "zoi .gy. Get out of bed! .gy." is a sound. In any case, from the
point of view of a Lojban speaker, "zoi .gy. Get out of bed! .gy." is
indeed nothing but a sound (or a string of letters). Meaningwise it is
no different from "zoi .gy. mumble mumble .gy.", unless they happen to
understand English.

> but what about
> {mi krixa se du'u zoi .gy. Get out of bed! .gy.}?  Also wrong, right?

That has a couple of grammatical problems. After "se du'u" you need a
full sentence, and "zoi .gy. Get out of bed! .gy." is only a sumti.
Also you would normally use "lo se du'u ...", since "se du'u .... kei"
is a selbri.

"la'e zoi .gy. Get out of bed! .gy." is the meaning of "zoi .gy. Get
out of bed! .gy.", (which will only be meaningful to English speakers,
of course, not necessarily to any Lojban speaker), but as you point
out you don't really krixa a meaning.

You could say something like "mi ki'asku lu ko cliva lo ckana li'u".

mu'o mi'e xorxes

What we usually use for this is "kixsku" (also sometimes spelled
"ki'asku") which is from krixa + cusku (to say) and means x1 cries out
expression x2 to audience x3 through medium x4.

With "du'u" the first place is a predication, a meaning, and the
second place is a sentence that expresses that meaning.  Neither of
those fit directly with the second place of "krixa", which is a sound.
