I attempted to say something along the lines of:
My university's library does not have <a particular book>.
preferably without having to use {pe}. My attempt was using a somewhat long
lujvo, seljitckusro, which I defined as:
x1 is a library containing books x2 managed by x3.
(The underlying tanru was {se jitro ke cukta sorcu}.)
and then the construction (with the book being the last sumti of the
previous sentence) was:
.i ri na se seljitckusro fi lo balcu'e be fo mi
Does this seem reasonable? I don't have much practice coining lujvo but am
attempting to get better at it.

The second one seems wrong to me, because the school itself is not a library
in general, and so even if the book in question was one they had the
sentence would still be true. And I dunno, {ckusro} seems like the better
relation to use for "having a book" in the sense of a library, better than
{ponse}, at least; the kind of possession of a book that a library is
involved in seems somewhat different from generic possession. Also, as I
said I was trying to avoid {pe} (and the nesting structure that is
equivalent to it) as best as possible; I find it useful for things that
aren't compact otherwise but prefer to avoid it when possible.

mu'o mi'e latros.
