On Oct 1, 2010, at 11:24, Alex Rozenshteyn wrote:

> Is there a way to say something like "So you say that I am fat?"
>
> The problem is that cusku takes a (sedu'u/text/lu'e concept), and

"se du'u" is what you want, not "a more general abstraction".

Without considering the English "So ...?" construct, this is:

   do   cusku  lo se du'u  mi    plana
   You  say    that        I  am fat

"lo se du'u" is, in general, what you use where you could use a  
quotation but are instead expressing the concept in your own words.  
"You" said something whose meaning was "I am fat", but you're not  
saying what words were said.

> gah, du'u and sedu'u so are so closely related that it's hard to distinguish

I'm not sure if this is confusing you or not, but let me just mention that 
{se du'u} isn't a separate abstractor, it's just a handy way of getting at the
x2 of the relationship. {du'u} (like all abstractors) takes a bridi and
converts it into a selbri, in this case with the definition "x1 is predication
[bridi] expressed in sentence x2". So

du'u mi plana [vau] [kei]

is "x1 is the predication of my being fat, which can be expressed in sentence
x2".

Also, don't overlook {xusra}, which takes a du'u1 natively.

.i do xusra lo du'u mi plana [vau] [kei] [ku] [vau]

On Oct 1, 2010, at 15:03, Alex Rozenshteyn wrote:

> Because I thought {se du'u} was the wrong thing...
>
> Let me work through this:
> du'u : x1 is a predicate expressed by sentence x2
> se du'u : x1 is a sentence expressing predicate x2

Yes, except:

   1. bridi/statement/sentence, not predicate/selbri. "plana" is a
      selbri (predicate); "do plana" is a bridi (sentence).

   2. {du'u} relates three things, not two: x1, x2, and the syntactic
      content of the {du'u} itself.

Or, in lojbanic tautologies:

   lo du'u <anything> kei bridi
   (the x1 of du'u is a bridi)

   lo du'u mi broda do kei bridi la'e zo broda mi ce'o do
   ("mi broda do" is a bridi with selbri "broda" and sumti "mi", "do")

   da du'u <anything> kei de .ijanai da smuni de
   (the x2 of a du'u means the x1 of it)

On Fri, Oct 1, 2010 at 5:35 PM, Alex Rozenshteyn <rpglover64@gmail.com> wrote:
>
> I think my problem is understanding multiple places in abstractors.

It might help to consider some bridi that use them in an ordinary way.
 Let's take for example "si'o", the idea abstractor.  It has two
places, the x1 is the idea, and the x2 is who thinks it.  What's
inside is what the idea is about.  So let's make it that the sky is
blue: "si'o lo tsani cu blanu kei".  Now that we've got that selbri,
we can use it normally.  The x1 is the idea that the sky is blue, and
the x2 is someone who thinks the sky is blue.  These are all the same:

.i si'o lo tsani cu blanu kei fa lo sidbo fe lo te sidbo
.i lo sidbo cu si'o lo tsani cu blanu kei lo te sidbo
.i lo te sidbo cu se si'o lo tsani cu blanu kei lo sidbo
.i lo sidbo lo te sidbo cu si'o lo tsani cu blanu [kei]
.i lo te sidbo lo sidbo cu se si'o lo tsani cu blanu [kei]

Or I could say for instance "si'o lo tsani cu blanu kei mi", that puts
"mi" in the x2 so it says "I have an idea that the sky is blue".
Let's take another example, Alice had a good idea that we should go to
the market:

.i si'o ma'a klama lo zarci kei fa lo xamgu fe la .alis.
.i lo xamgu cu si'o ma'a klama lo zarci kei la .alis.
.i la .alis. se si'o ma'a klama lo zarci kei lo xamgu
.i lo xamgu la .alis. si'o ma'a klama lo zarci [kei]
.i la .alis. lo xamgu cu se si'o ma'a klama lo zarci [kei]

Here's a common Lojbanic saying that's an example of a straightforward
bridi using "li'i" (I think maybe it was la xalbo who thought of it?):
"lo melbi ko li'i cerni", have a beautiful morning.  The two places of
"li'i" are x1 is the experience of the embedded bridi, and x2 is the
experiencer who experiences it.  So "lo melbi" is the experience, and
"ko" is the experiencer, so it tells you to have an experience that's
beautiful.

Actually I think Timo is right.  {sei} is funny in that it will take as many
sumti as you give it, followed by selbri and then that's it.

Trailing sumti are attached to the parent bridi.  So if I say {mi nelci sei
la tam cusku lo nu citka} I have said:  "I like, tom said, eating".  Not "I
like, tom said the event of eating".  If you wanted to say that you would
have to say {mi nelci sei la tam lo nu citka cu cusku}. Whatever that might
mean, the {lo nu} there belongs to the cusku.  The other way is to use {be}
like Timo does.
