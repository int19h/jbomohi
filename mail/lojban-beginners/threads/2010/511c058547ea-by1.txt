Does jbofi'e parse BY1 correctly? This is what it says:

--------------------------------
.i ga'e broda to'a

*.i*
*`.'* [1(2*[broda1 (predicate var 1??)
:] **ga'e*
*upper-case shift*)2
*[is, does]* <<3*broda*
*predicate var 1??*>>3 (4*[broda2 (predicate var 1??)
:] **to'a*
*lower-case shift*)4]1

--------------------------------

{ga'e} and {to'a} are {broda}'s x1 and x2?

Another example:

--------------------------------
.i ce'a lerf. bu broda


*.i*
*`.'* [1(2*[broda1 (predicate var 1??)
:] **ce'a*
*font shift* *lerf.bu*
*?*)2
*[is, does]* <<3*broda*
*predicate var 1??*>>3]1

--------------------------------

Is {ce'a lerfu. bu} supposed to be a sumti?

> {ga'e} and {to'a} are {broda}'s x1 and x2?
.skamyxatra

Yes, they are.  Any string of BY {cmavo} is a {lerfu} string, which can serve
as a pro-{sumti}.  The fact that the {cmavo} are in BY1 and not BY2 is
irrelevant to the grammar; sub-{selma'o} exist only to indicate semantic
divisions to humans.

> Is {ce'a lerfu. bu} supposed to be a sumti?

Yes.  A LAU and a {lerfu} word together form a {lerfu} word, which can always
be used as a one-element {lerfu} string, and thus as a pro-{sumti}, regardless
of whether it makes any semantic sense.
