> xu do viska lome la'e zoi gy death metal gy jipci skina
> Did you see the Death Metal Rooster video?

The sentence is grammatical. But it doesn't specify temporality, so it
can mean not only "Did you see ..." but also "Do you plan on seeing
...", "Are you seeing ...", and so on. For your example, I suggest
{ba'o}.

{viska} means optical perception; so, if I catch some snippets of the
video on YouTube but don't watch the whole of it, that would still be
the case of {viska}. You might want to consider {zgana}, "observe".
Note that you can also use {ve skina} for {to watch a video}.

I'd have to know more about the Death Metal rooster to know if you said what 
you meant. "lo me la'e zoi gy. death metal .gy. jipci skina" means a movie 
about (or somehow related to) a rooster which has something to do with Death 
Metal. The rooster has to actually be a rooster (or a hen, or a hunk of 
chicken meat...) (which doesn't imply it has to be a real rooster; it could 
be a rooster that someone drew as a Death Metal logo...). If it's about a 
person, involved with Death Metal, who is nicknamed "the Death Metal 
Rooster", then you want "lo me la me zoi .gy. death metal .gy. jipci ku 
skina".
