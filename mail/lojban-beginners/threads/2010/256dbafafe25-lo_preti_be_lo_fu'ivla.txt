lo gismu na fu'ivla. CCVVCV is proposed in the Book as a form for cultural 
fu'ivla, but we've made up cultural fu'ivla in other forms too, such 
as "katlana" (Catalan) and "nienke'a" (Mohawk). (I say "we", but both of 
those are by me. I'd have to check jbovlaste to see what anyone else has made 
up.) Another one in CCVVCV form is "mraigo" (Welsh).

The place structure of a type-4 cultural fu'ivla is like that of "glico" 
or "fraso". The place structure of a type-3 is like that of the gismu it's 
made from, so if you want one with the same place structure as "bolgaro", 
it's "slovnblgaria", not "kulnrblgaria".

"fi lo fu'ivla pe lo kulnu"; "zo glico".

=== 5 | 2010-10-21T02:51:09Z | Pierre Abbat <phma@phma.optus.nu> | 201010202251.12114.phma@phma.optus.nu | mail/lojban-beginners/cur/1287629469.37fd0d749338dc5d.jbomohi:2,S
On Wednesday 20 October 2010 13:33:01 Geoffrey Stewart wrote:
> 2 more questions:
>
> I have seen in places the type IV fu'ivla proposed to be used in a rafsi
> form. How does that work?

There are two methods in use, and they can be mixed. Jorge's method works for 
all fu'ivla; mine works for an infinite class, but not all, and often 
produces shorter lujvo.

Jorge's method uses "'y" after the last vowel in the fu'ivla and before the 
next rafsi: anmonia + djacu -> anmonia'yjau. If the fu'ivla begins with a 
vowel and you want to put a rafsi before it, add "'": mikri + enri = 
miky'enri.

My method drops the final vowel, as when forming the four-letter rafsi of a 
gismu, and inserts "y" when joining a fu'ivla to a following rafsi. To join a 
preceding rafsi to a fu'ivla, make sure the rafsi ends in a consonant, and 
insert "y" regardless of phonotactics. Fu'ivla that end in more than one 
vowel (including with apostrophes) cannot be joined to a following rafsi this 
way, and some fu'ivla (mu'u zo skalduna fa'u zo malgaci) cannot appear at the 
beginning of a lujvo because they turn into lo valsrslinku'i fa'u gimjvo.

> Is there a list of fu'ivla anywhere?

http://jbovlaste.lojban.org/dict/listing.html?type=fu%27ivla
There are some fu'ivla that aren't listed; for instance, five months of the 
Roman calendar (martio, prilio, madjio djunio, djulio) are missing because of 
a bug in jbofi'e.

  Your first sentence, while grammatically fine, can be simplified as
"mi te preti lo fu'ivla".  Using se/te/ve/xe to change the order of
arguments of a brivla can be a useful tool.  Your second sentence is
not grammatical, since "fi" must be followed by a sumti, as does pe,
so "ma vlaturge'a fi lo fu'ivla pe lo kulnu".  Your third sentence is
fine.  I'm not quite sure what you are trying to do with "pamai loi
gismu noi lu'e glico cu vlaturge'a", but it's not grammatical (lu'e
has to be followed by a sumti, but even with an added lo before glico,
it's not what you are trying to say).  Try this-- ".i pamai go'i loi
gismu be mu'u zo glico" (firstly, [I observe] gismu, such as 'glico').
 Simliarly with the next two sentences.  Your last sentence is fine.
