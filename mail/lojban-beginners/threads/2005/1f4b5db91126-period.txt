On 10/16/05, Naomi K <alien.juxtaposition@gmail.com> wrote:
>
> Is it allowable to use the lojban period to the same purpose as an English
> comma (show pauses for the reader)?


Nope. The Lojban period is a kind of phonological/morphological entity. It's
there to help retain semantic unambiguity in a text string.

There's the "it's taking me a while to think up what to say" pause { .y. }
or { .yyyyyy. } But Lojban makes the grammatical structure so clear to the
reader that nothing like the European comma is really needed.

On Sun, Oct 16, 2005 at 07:34:23PM +1000, Naomi K wrote:
> Is it allowable to use the lojban period to the same purpose as an
> English comma (show pauses for the reader)?

In fact, that is the only thing that a Lojban period *can* indicate:
it means "pause here".

However, it really shouldn't be used for hesitation pauses (that's
.y.) or to group clauses (that's not done with pausing in Lojban).
