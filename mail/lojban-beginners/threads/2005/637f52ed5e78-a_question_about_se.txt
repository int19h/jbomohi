sumti can be as complex as needed (including SE-conversions).
If you want to say "the big translated text", you need {se} in your compound 
sumti : {le barda se fanva} ({le barda fanva} would be "the big translator").
So {se} will never do the job of {cu}.

In other words, if {cu} is omitted here, {se tcika} would be considered as a
part of the first sumti.

No, it wouldn't. If we drop {cu} in the above sentence then  {klama}
and {se tcika} will glue together to form a single selbri (tanru)
{klama se tcika}. Besides, {cu}  terminates the abstraction started by
{nu}. Without it, {la daucac} comes under the scope of {nu}.
Try the two versions (with {cu} amd without) by jbofi'e and see the difference.

Well, it modifies a "selbri", but not necessarily the whole selbri of
the bridi--it only modifies the next brivla-or-equivalent following
it. But you can use it to modify a whole complex selbri, like this:

le nu mi klama cu se ke drani tcika la daucac.

According to CLL (5.11) this is a bit different in meaning than

le nu mi klama cu drani se tcika la daucac.

though the main selbri has the same place structure. Exactly how the
meaning differs, I'm not really sure though. Whether anyone makes this
kind of distinction in practice, I don't know.
