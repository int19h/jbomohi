I want to be able to say:

Line "a" is perpendicular with line "b".

Create a line that is perpendicular with line "k" and includes point "C".

The radical line of two circles is perpendicular with the line that 
includes their centers.

----

The only thing I found related to perpendicular was kurfa.
What is a practical example for kurfa's place structure?
In what order do we list the vertices in x2?

kurfa
x1 is a right-angled shape/form defined by set of vertices x2, dimensions 
x3 (default 2).


----

For geometric construction, my opening suggestion would be:

x1 (line) defines four equal angles (is perpendicular) with line x2 and 
includes point(s) x3 and intersects "x2" at x4

This is similar to panra (if panra can accept lines for x1 and x2, and a 
point (or points) for x3).

panra
x1 parallels x2 differing only in property x3 (ka; jo'u/fa'u term) by 
standard/geometry x4.

> I want to be able to say:
>
> Line "a" is perpendicular with line "b".

I'd go with "sraji":

lo linji po'u .abu cu sraji lo linji po'u by

Create a line that is perpendicular with line "k" and includes point "C".
>

ko zmadu lo linji poi sraji lo linji po'u ky gi'e vasru lo mokca po'u cy


> The radical line of two circles is perpendicular with the line that
> includes their centers.
>

lo [radical line] be lo re cukla cu sraji lo linji be lo re midju be cy


> For geometric construction, my opening suggestion would be:
>
> x1 (line) defines four equal angles (is perpendicular) with line x2 and
> includes point(s) x3 and intersects "x2" at x4
>

I wouldn't restrict it to lines. A lline can be perpendicular to a plane,
and also it should be usable with general objects, as in:

  lo grana cu sraji lo bitmu
  The pole is perpendicular to the wall.

For your full description I think it's more clear to use three separate
predicates:

  ko'a linji ko'e gi'e sraji ko'i gi'e kruca ko'i ko'o

But if you must, you could make it into a lujvo: kucrajli'i

mu'o mi'e xorxes
