I want to be able to say:

Line "a" is perpendicular with line "b".

Create a line that is perpendicular with line "k" and includes point "C".

The radical line of two circles is perpendicular with the line that 
includes their centers.

----

The only thing I found related to perpendicular was kurfa.
What is a practical example for kurfa's place structure?
In what order do we list the vertices in x2?

kurfa
x1 is a right-angled shape/form defined by set of vertices x2, dimensions 
x3 (default 2).


----

For geometric construction, my opening suggestion would be:

x1 (line) defines four equal angles (is perpendicular) with line x2 and 
includes point(s) x3 and intersects "x2" at x4

This is similar to panra (if panra can accept lines for x1 and x2, and a 
point (or points) for x3).

panra
x1 parallels x2 differing only in property x3 (ka; jo'u/fa'u term) by 
standard/geometry x4.

I'd go with "sraji":

lo linji po'u .abu cu sraji lo linji po'u by

Create a line that is perpendicular with line "k" and includes point "C".
>

ko zmadu lo linji poi sraji lo linji po'u ky gi'e vasru lo mokca po'u cy


> The radical line of two circles is perpendicular with the line that
> includes their centers.
>

lo [radical line] be lo re cukla cu sraji lo linji be lo re midju be cy


> For geometric construction, my opening suggestion would be:
>
> x1 (line) defines four equal angles (is perpendicular) with line x2 and
> includes point(s) x3 and intersects "x2" at x4
>

I wouldn't restrict it to lines. A lline can be perpendicular to a plane,
and also it should be usable with general objects, as in:

  lo grana cu sraji lo bitmu
  The pole is perpendicular to the wall.

For your full description I think it's more clear to use three separate
predicates:

  ko'a linji ko'e gi'e sraji ko'i gi'e kruca ko'i ko'o

But if you must, you could make it into a lujvo: kucrajli'i

mu'o mi'e xorxes

I don't know, but the default value would be the Earth. When x2 is a line
or a plane then "vertical" in its gravity field (assuming a uniform mass
density) would correspond to "perpendicular", so I think it's a reasonable
choice of word for "perpendicular".


xorxes: I wouldn't restrict it to lines. A line can be perpendicular to a

I'm not sure what the advantage would be of having a separate word, but
brivla space is infinite, so it is possible to create words as nuanced as
one wants. If people will bother to learn and stick to such fine
distinctions is another issue. The same will happen with all geometric
words. Can a cookie be cukla, or do we need one word for circle and a
separate word for circular object? The gismu list is not very consistent
here (as usual). For example "bolci" has a place for its material, so
apparently it excludes the abstract sphere.

One advantage would be clarity.

In my life, vertical is a simple observational approximation.
Vertical things are approximately parallel with a standing human,
or plumb line.

x1 seems vertical (straight up and down) to observer x2

If an infinite line and infinite plane are conceptual objects,
then two of them forming all equal angles is a unique conceptual 
relationship. 
Applying gravity seems forced to me.

Based on your examples it seems like we only need two places:

x1 forms all equal angles (is perpendicular) with x2

I would probably create another word for:

x1 forms a 90 degree angle with x2
