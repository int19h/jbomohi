Robin,

a meaningful sentence that ignores semantics can be humorous if it
does so based on the listener's expectation. The expectation in this
case is based on an English idiom.

This is why Groucho Marx was humorous when he said "Outside of a dog,
a book is a man's best friend. Inside of a dog it's too dark to read."
It may be difficult for Commander Data or a Lojban speaker to
understand this, but this is a form of humor.

It depends on what you mean by that. Your sentence literally means
Heath is the same as one male humanoid person. It would be a bad habit
to use "mintu" as a substitute for "is." To get into a Lojbanic frame
of mind you must leave behind the need for the word "is." For
instance, if you want to say "Heath is a male humanoid person" you can
just say {xit. nanmu}.

-epkat

On 1/7/06, Heath N. Caldwell <hncaldwell@csupomona.edu> wrote:
> It is not sufficient to just describe Heath as being an exemplar of a
> group.  The purpose is to say that there is one and only one "The man",
> and that Heath is he.

Notice that you want {du} here, not mintu. The x1 and x2
of mintu are two identical (by some standard) but separate
things. For example {la xit mintu la djan lo ka makau jvinu
ce'u}, "Heath is identical to John in how they look". Heath
and John are identical in how they look, but Heath is not
John. If you want to relate two sumti with the same referent,
then you have to use {du}: {la xit du le pa nanmu} "Heath
is the one man (i.e. the one man I have in mind)".
