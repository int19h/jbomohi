What about lujvo on form CCVCCVCCVCV or CCVCCVCCVCCV etc.? In a 4-
syllable word it's hard not to stress the first syllable, so they
become unrecognizable from tanru CCVCCV CCVCV and CCVCCV CCVCCV. The
CLL doesn't force any pause in such tanru according to http://dag.github.com/cll/4/9/
and there is no rule to insert a hyphen in the middle of the lujvo (in
fact, words in form CCVCCVrCCVCCV like *{xlagrurblakla} are recognized
by vlatai as bad fu'ivla).
Correct me if I'm wrong.

You just have to be careful when pronouncing such words. Fortunately, getting 
the stress or juncture wrong in a word like this will only add the ambiguity 
that a tanru has, or change the grouping, not change the meaning to something 
totally different as dropping the r-hyphen would. A real example which 
doesn't quite fit your form is "baupla fuzykamni". Drop the stress on "bau" 
and you get "bauplafuzykamni", which is just a single word with the same 
morphemes.

Here's a stress exercise: lo jbojbe na lojbo jbena lo jbojbena.

An r-hyphen can be inserted only after a CVV or CV'V rafsi. The wordoid you 
gave is a slinku'i: x-lag-rur-bla-kla. What is a xlagrublakla, anyway?

=== 3 | 2011-01-18T02:32:40Z | ianek <janek37@gmail.com> | 07d4e952-8675-4719-b366-667abd8bd931@s5g2000yqm.googlegroups.com | mail/lojban-beginners/cur/1295317960.95bff787688d238c.jbomohi:2,S
Thanks for a fun example!
CLL forces a pause between {lo} and {jbojbena} in case you stress
{lo}. This pause is here because it's sometimes hard not to stress a
cmavo before a brivla. The case in my question is similar. If I ever
need to say "xlagru blakla", I'll try to remember to put a pause
between (as it's always acceptable).
Actually, {bauplafuzykamni} is two words: {bau plafuzykamni}.
{xlagrublakla} is a lujvo I made to get a handy example. I guess it's
a bad-grainish-blue-goer.
