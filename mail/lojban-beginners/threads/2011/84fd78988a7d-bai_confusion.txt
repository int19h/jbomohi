=== 2 | 2011-04-12T15:49:51Z | Luke Bergen <lukeabergen@gmail.com> | banlktimqa_q5_cmvhn2xergjhxjift=qva@mail.gmail.com | mail/lojban-beginners/cur/1302623391.7da3df2b66d759d0.jbomohi:2,S
I've been confused by anything other than plain vanilla use of BAI on a
number of occasions myself.

The way I've come to think of them (and tags in general) is to think of them
as marking the following sumti as being related to the bridi/whatever by the
tag.

i.e.  {bai lo gerku} = "the dog (which is related to the bridi in a
'compelled by' kind of way)".  Or {mi citka lo plise ba lo nu mi klama lo
zdani} is saying that the sumti {lo nu mi klama lo zdani} is related to the
bridi in a "ba" kind of way (that is, the bridi is {ba} to the sumti).

Likewise, if I see BAI coming before a sumti I think of it like "ok, here
comes a sumti, but it's a kind that is related to (whatever) in a BAI kind
of way".

So I would read {mi ne tedu'o lo mikce saske} as "{mi ne lo mikce saske (but
the sumti lo mikce saske is related to mi in a tedu'o kind of way)}" => "I
who am knowing about the doctor science am able to help" => "I know about
medical science, I can help".

I'm on pretty unstable mental ground with regard to TAGs though, so if any
{jbocre} think that I'm off please speak up.

Where did you see this by the way?
