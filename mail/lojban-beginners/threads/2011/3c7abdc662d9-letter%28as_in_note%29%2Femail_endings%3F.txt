It appears that common practice for ending emails is to have {mu'o mi'e
_______ }  where "________" is the name, but I recently noticed an email
signature in which the writer used {mi'e ________ mu'o} which, once I
thought about it, seems to make more sense. If {mu'o} is supposed to signal
the end of the transmission, anything after that would be disregarded, and
the self-introduction conventionally put at the end of emails and letters,
if put after the {mu'o} instead of before, would seem to be excluded from
the message. So when I actually think about it, it seems like {mi'e
_________ mu'o} would make more sense than the more common {mu'o mi'e
________ } I more commonly see. I'm wondering whether my friend has has an
insight missed by the majority of the most vocal lojbanists, or if there is
some reason for the common form that I have not yet considered.

.i mi'e la .jdakrat.skaryzgik. mu'o

=== 2 | 2011-04-24T01:58:30Z | Alex Rozenshteyn <rpglover64@gmail.com> | banlkti=xonpg6vqzlxkqdfrr-6_r6z_dpq@mail.gmail.com | mail/lojban-beginners/cur/1303610310.96d21085abb88e88.jbomohi:2,S
{mu'o} doesn't signal the end of a transmission. It signals that the next
word is the person/group you're talking to (although it can be prematurely
terminated with {do'u}), and that you are then done.  {mi'e} means that the
next word is your name.

{mi'e ___ mu'o} may have another word following it, indicating whom it was
directed to.
{mu'o mi'e ___} has nothing following it, and doesn't explicitly say whom
it's directed to.

"mu'o" and "mi'e" are both vocatives. The sumti (you can also have a selbri or 
a bare name; in each of these cases an article is assumed) after "mu'o" is 
the person you're yielding the floor to; the sumti after "mi'e" is yourself. 
In "mu'o mi'e pier" the object of "mu'o" is an implicit "zo'e", so I'm 
yielding the floor to anyone. In "mi'e pier. mu'o" the object of "mu'o" is 
any sumti, selbri, or bare name that comes after it; if there is none, it's 
implicit "zo'e".
