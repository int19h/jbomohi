On Thu, Dec 11, 2008 at 7:52 PM, Luke Bergen <lukeabergen@gmail.com> wrote:
> Is it legitimate to use attitudinals as regular words?

(They are regular words. I think you mean as brivla.) No, they can't
be used as brivla directly.

> ie. is the following
> lojban sentence acceptable?
>
> xu do .ui
>
> or does this just mean "are you <I'm happy>"?

It means "are you?" or "do you?" (or "were you?", or "did you?", or
...) depending on the understood predicate, plus an expression of
happiness from the speaker.

You could however use an attitudinal to construct a zei-lujvo, for example:

 xu do ui zei co'e
 "Are you ui-ish?"

You've got it backwards. UI attach to the preceding word or structure; if it
appears at the beginning it applies to the whole bridi (See Reference
Grammar 13:1 <http://jbotcan.org/cllc/c13/s1.html>). If, in speaking, you
don't realize you wish to express a full-bridi UI until you've already
started, you can close the bridi with {vau} & then add the UI to similar
effect.

The question word for UI is {pei}, so {.ui pei} translates loosely to "Are
you happy?" (See Reference Grammar 13:10<http://jbotcan.org/cllc/c13/s10.html>
.)
