OK so this is how I'm beginning to understand it: "zo'o" modifies an  
attitudinal to say "I'm pretending to feel this way, in what I'm  
saying, but it's not my real feeling."  For instance: "First thing  
today my car got towed, so that was a great start to the day.  
[.uizo'o]"  Just kidding about being happy-- the speaker is serious  
that their car got towed, just not that they feel good about it.  Your  
example ".oizo'o" is like "I'm pretending I feel hurt, for fun, but  
really I'm fine."  Or you could say for instance ".a'azo'o"-- "oh  
yeah, I'm TOTALLY paying attention, sure."

By that way of understanding, it seems to me that the common use of  
zo'o on its own could be seen as modifying an implied ".ia" or ".ie"--  
that is, the speaker is kidding about whether they even believe or  
agree with what they're saying.
