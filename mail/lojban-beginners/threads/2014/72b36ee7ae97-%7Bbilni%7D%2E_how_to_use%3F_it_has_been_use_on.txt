> If yes, jbovlaste says it's a government of the US whereas it should be "a

> {naku lo prenu ka'e turni} logically because {naku lo prenu ka'e ciste}.

turni1 is something which governs a populace a/o territory. It makes no
distinguishment about whether that something is a person, system, or
anything else which can govern. It is quite possible that in some
instances, lo turni is a person, while in others, it is a system.

mertru, on the other hand, means "the government of the US" because when
the lujvo was created, the person making it obviously decided against
including ciste for brevity's sake. Including ciste in the lujvo-making
would have doubled the syllable count with no real benefit, as all it does
is explicitly mark that it refers to a system, which anyone who knows that
the U.S. is a republic would already know. Lujvo are /not/ required to mean
exactly the same thing that the tanru they are built from would mean. On
that note, the office of the president of the U.S. is {lo merja'a}.


> Another question: should bilni1 be an integral part of bilni2? Is it a
> part of American government born by it and now a part of it or merely an
> entity that gasnu or se jalge it?
>

The National Guard is a part of the military, which is a part of the
executive branch of the government. One could therefore argue that, in this
particular example, bilni1 is a subset of bilni2, but I won't go so far as
to say that's always the case with every use of bilni.

> I can see bilni in two ways.

1) bende with the x2 removed, plus the usual connotations of things being
military. (That being said, I would like to see bende2 removed, since it's
just another instance of group membership which can be achieved by other
means.)
2) the definition is a bit off. Read it instead like this: "bilni = x1 is
*a* military/regimented/strongly organized x2 for purpose x3". This way,
bilni is what I call a "connotation selbri", like the cultural gismu. This
allows you to use it as a compositional adjective, like mutce et al. e.g.
bende bilni -> bilni lo ka ce'u bende -> "army". You can effectively
"militarize" any selbri by sticking "bilni" onto the end thus.

Regardless of these two interpretations, bilni3 is just a property of the
x1 that expresses the raison-d'etre of the x1. It's pretty redundant to
mukti, pe'i.

.i tu poi sonci certu so'u mei cu bilni lo ka bende kei lo ka ce'u nurgau
lo noltruti'u lo palci mafcre
"These few expert soldiers are a military group destined to rescue the
princess from the evil witch."
