Lojban allows on to make lujvo from pre-existing cmavo, and gismu.
For the sake of cognitive efficiency, is there a way to make new lujvo from 
cmavo, gismu and
pre-existing lujvo?  I do not think this is allowed, since we do not know 
the rafsi for the newly
created lujvo.

For example, suppose I wish to create deoxyribonucleic acid lujvo.
First create lujvo for an acid (chemistry), then an OH group (organic 
chemistry), then nucleotide (biology), ...
you get the idea.
Now, combine these lujvo results into a new lujvo.
I could generalize this procedure to mathematics, electrical 
engineering, theater, and so on.
Thanks.

=== 2 | 2014-04-22T20:57:28Z | la durka <durka42@gmail.com> | b82b3fe0-d3d9-4948-9689-18a88101dad9@googlegroups.com | mail/lojban-beginners/cur/1398200248.5424b4080034d618.jbomohi:2,S
In short: yes, and it's the same procedure you already know.

Lujvo are made of rafsi, so getting the rafsi is not a problem. In the 
simple case, you can just glom the lujvo together. Consider the lujvo 
{jungau} = "to tell/make known". Now let's make a lujvo for a person who 
does that -- maybe it means a town crier, or an incorrigible gossip. So 
this will be a lujvo from {jungau prenu}. All we have to do is choose a 
rafsi for {prenu} and stick them together: {jungaupre}. (Or {jungauprenu}, 
or {djunygaupre}, etc... by definition they are all the same word.)

Potential complications:
- When making long tanru, you put in {ke ... ke'e} as necessary to get the 
grouping right. These will sometimes show up in long lujvo as well.
- You have to watch out for tosmabru. But you have to do that when making 
lujvo out of gismu anyway.

(ta'o there is apparently already a lujvo for DNA, and it's made from 
gismu: {ginxu'i}, from {jgina xukmi}.)
