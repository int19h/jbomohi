On 27 August 2014 16:12, TR NS <transfire@gmail.com> wrote:

> Let me ask a follow-up question. How would something like "caio" (sha-yo)
> be interpreted? I assume it will be considered two cmavo, "ca" and "io".
> But then I (almost) never see a word like "io" used anywhere but at the
> beginning of a sentence and never with out a clear stop `.io`.
>

It can only be interpreted as {ca io}, and the dot before the word "io" is
not necessary, since the approximant there is a consonant, not a vowel. In
other words, it's the sound /j/ and not /i/, despite the spelling. The
appearance of this consonant forces the beginning of a new syllable (all
syllables begin with a consonant), and the word boundary is determined by
the lack of stress / consonant clusters around.

This is confirmed by the camxes parser, which properly implements the
morphology.

http://camxes.lojban.org/?text=caio

The "all syllables must begin with a consonant" rule is the reason for
putting dots before words that (would otherwise) start with a vowel, such
as {.a}: the dot is a consonant.
