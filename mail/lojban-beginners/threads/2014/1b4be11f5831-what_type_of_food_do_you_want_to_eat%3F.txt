English pattern:

Examples for review:

What type of food do you want to eat?

pei citka ma

When do you want to leave?
pei cliva ca ma

How do you want to get there?
pei klama fo ma

Why do you want to take that street?
do zmanei zo'e ki'u ma

Who do you want to pick up first?
???

preference?

I always have doubts about that use of "poi" because it involves a kind of
metalinguistic restriction rather than an ordinary restrictive clause. It
restricts the set of possible answers, but the straightforward application
of the poi and ma rules should be that "ma" is asking for a sumti which,
when restricted by the poi-clause, fits the argument place. So I would
prefer "ma noi" here. Similarly with demonstratives "ti poi", etc. "

With {ti poi} I would agree that it's off, but if we were to rewrite {do 
djica tu'a ma poi klesi lo cidja} as {ma klesi lo cidja gi'e poi'i do 
djica tu'a ke'a} ("What is a kind of food and is such that you want [to 
eat] it?"), it seems to have the right semantics.

Is {ma} not much closer to {da} than it is to something like {ti}? The 
weird (but often useful) thing about {ma} is that it always has highest 
scope, so it should be able to parallel {da} (as in {su'o da poi broda 
cu brode} -> {su'o da broda gi'e brode}.

On Friday, December 26, 2014 22:35:09 ravas wrote:
> The most recent thing learning lojban has made me more aware of is the
> English pattern:
> ... do you want to ...
> 
> Examples for review:
> 
> What type of food do you want to eat?
> do djica lo ma cidja

I'd say "lo mo cidja". "lo ma cidja", as already stated, means "whose food".

> What do you want to eat?
> pei citka ma

"do djica lo nu citka ma" or "audai citka ma". "pei citka ma" is a double 
question, "How do you feel about eating what?".

> Where do you want to eat?
> pei citka tu'i ma

"audai citka bu'u ma". Besides what xorxes said, "bu'u" is a tense word, 
whereas "tu'i" is a proper preposition. (The syntactic distinction is that 
proper prepositions can be preceded by SE; semantically, all proper 
prepositions are equivalent to "fi'o <brivla>", except for "do'e" which is "fi'o 
co'e".)

> Why do you want to take that street?
> do zmanei zo'e ki'u ma

do klama fo ta poi klaji ku'o .audai ki'u ma
and there are three other ways to ask "why": "mu'i ma", "seja'e ma", and "ni'i 
ma".

Pierre
