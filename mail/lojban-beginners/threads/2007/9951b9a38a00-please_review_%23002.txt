=== 2 | 2007-04-20T18:49:09Z | Daniel Adamec <dadamec123@gmail.com> | 241e6b720704201149h6dd9d805u95bc2555db331965@mail.gmail.com | mail/lojban-beginners/cur/1177094949.85e09192654c0f61.jbomohi:2,S
vecnu is "x1 [seller] sells/vends x2 [goods/service/commodity] to buyer x3
for amount/cost/expense x4"
so "se vecnu" is the thing being sold. If I understand this correctly
(admittedly I am not very experienced) te vecnu would be the buyer.
terve'u (x1 buys x2 [goods/service/commodity] from x3 [seller] for
amount/cost/expense x4) may be a more compact way of putting it.
"klama se vecnu" seems like it should mean "go-type-of-sold-thing". "mu'i"
or another similar cmavo might be useful.
I might also suggest the addition of an article at the beginning (*le* mi
mamta) ("le mamta *be* mi" might also work ). "mi mamta" sounds like *you*
are the mother (mamta - x1 is a mother of x2).
I don't know about the use of "makau" but that's probably just a slip on my
part.
I do consider myself a beginner so everything here is subject to be
overridden by a more experienced lojbanist. "Taken with a grain of salt" so
to speak
co'o mi'e danixel.

few things slightly differently, to wit:

lo mamta be mi pu klama te zu'e lo nu terve'u lo jisra be lo plise ja
najnimre ca lo nu la .djeikyb. stali lo zdani .i mi ba terve'u lo jikru ba'o

mistakes like missing {cu}, dropping abstractions, & the like.

mi mamta -> le mi mamta
se vecnu -> te vecnu
{klama te vecnu} is grammatical and makes sense, but the emphasis is 
different. The English means "My mother went, which has something to do with 
buying juice." The Lojban means "My mother bought juice, which has something 
to do with going."
seti'u -> ca'o le nu | .ica'obo
se vecnu -> te vecnu
makau -> lenu
{makau} is used in abstractions to make indirect questions.
The middle sentence of the Lojban is missing from the English.
