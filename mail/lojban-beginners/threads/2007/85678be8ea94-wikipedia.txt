Particularly, the article (Lojban grammar) is lacking descriptions of:

1. Morphology: lujvo, fu'ivla

2. Morphology: cmavo (and its subclasses)

3. Syntax and semantics: tanru (including the discussion about the
perceived difference between {ro'erbroda} and {brode broda})

4. Syntax and semantics: logical connectives

5. Syntax and semantics: modifiers (parentheses, vocatives,
discursives, attitudinals)

6. Syntax and semantics: questions

7. Syntax and semantics: tenses

8. Syntax and semantics: mathematics


As for the main article (Lojban), the following topics are not fully covered:

1. History: Initial development 1987-1997

2. Comparison with other constructed languages (including Esperanto)

3. Samples (both common and unique expressions)

4. Community (its population and activity, either inside or outside US)

I've briefly looked at it. In the brivla section I read "brivla may be 
identified by the following properties: [...] Start with a consonant".
Obviously, this is the case for gismu and lujvo. However, I've seen some 
fu'ivla that start with a vowel (for example, jbovlaste lists agbakate as a 
fu'ivla for avocado).

To be honest, when I saw a fu'ivla starting in a vowel for the first time I 
didn't believe that it was a valid fu'ivla. But now I'm pretty sure that 
fu'ivla may start with a vowel under certain circumstances.
