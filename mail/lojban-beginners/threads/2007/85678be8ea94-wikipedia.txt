I've briefly looked at it. In the brivla section I read "brivla may be 
identified by the following properties: [...] Start with a consonant".
Obviously, this is the case for gismu and lujvo. However, I've seen some 
fu'ivla that start with a vowel (for example, jbovlaste lists agbakate as a 
fu'ivla for avocado).

To be honest, when I saw a fu'ivla starting in a vowel for the first time I 
didn't believe that it was a valid fu'ivla. But now I'm pretty sure that 
fu'ivla may start with a vowel under certain circumstances.

The Reference Grammar chapter 4.7 (on making a stage 3 fu'ivla):
"If the first letter is not a consonant, modify the beginning so that
the word begins with a consonant, either by removing an initial vowel
or adding a suggestively chosen initial consonant."

e.g. Acer (the scientific name of maple trees) --> xaceru

Beginning with a vowel is ok for stage 1 & 2 fu'ivla as they are used
in distinct constructs like {me la'o sy. avocado sy.} or {me la
.abakatat.} respectively; but stage 3 fu'ivla are exposed directly in
a sentence with other words. If having a vowel at the beginning is
valid for all fu'ivla, will the unambiguity/easiness of parsing a
Lojban sentence be still always the case?

=== 8 | 2007-08-11T18:43:49Z | Jorge Llambías <jjllambias@gmail.com> | 925d17560708111143l2cca8616t44fa4c45e7686e31@mail.gmail.com | mail/lojban-beginners/cur/1186857829.812801d5accc2477.jbomohi:2,S
This condition mentioned in the article for identifying brivla:

* Have a consonant cluster (at least two adjacent consonants) within
or between the first and second syllables

is also not quite right. For example consider {ba'armo'a}. The required
cluster occurs only between the second and third syllables.

CLL says that a consonant cluster is required within the first five
letters, but that is not really good either because it disallows
a form like ba'airba for no good reason (while allowing .a'airba
for example).

I would just say that the second consonant of a brivla (where the
apostrophe doesn't count as a consonant) must be part of a
consonant cluster.

But all of these focus on the necessary conditions for brivlahood,
not the sufficient conditions, as none of them deal with the tosmabru
and slinku'i issues.

Giving the necessary and sufficient conditions for brivla is not
very simple, as it in practice requires talking about rafsi.
