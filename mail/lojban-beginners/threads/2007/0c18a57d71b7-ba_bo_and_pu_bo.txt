Why do ba bo and pu bo mean opposites of what they should mean?

For example, la meris klama ba lenu mi klama fi le kumfa. (Mary came after I
left the room)
should be equivalent to - la meris klama .i babo mi klama le kumfa.
However (
http://www.tlg.uci.edu/~opoudjis/lojbanbrochure/lessons/less10connect.html)
says that
ba bo means "after wards." It also hints that this is because of its
similarity with baku, but it still bothers me.

-Anand

    Yes, I never really liked that. But it's basically the equivalent of
the English:

la meris klama ba lenu mi klama fi le kumfa  = Mary came after the event
of me-leaving-the-room

la meris klama .i babo mi klama fi le kumfa. =   Mary came.
After(wards) (or "After some unspecified event"), I left the room.

The "i" is key, making them two separate (but yet conneted because of
the bo) utterances.
