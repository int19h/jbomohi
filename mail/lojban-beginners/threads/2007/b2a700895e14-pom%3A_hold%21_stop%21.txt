Moments later Dotar Nors is dead and John Carter with bloody sword in  
hand is facing a circle of Green Men on thoats, when the Princess's  
air-yacht caroms off the top of the ridge behind them, passes over  
their heads, and scrapes to a crash-landing half a mile out on the  
plain.

TARS TARKAS

(to one warrior)

ko fanta lo nu ta rivbi

SUPER: Don't let him get away!

you! prevent the event of that-one escapes

(to the others)

ko kankla .i lo damba cu rinsa .ei

SUPER: Come! Battle calls!

you! accompany-go. battle greets (obligation)

The troop thunder off toward the crashed air-yacht. Carter shrugs and  
runs after on foot, able to go fast enough that his guard has to  
gallop to keep up. At the downed craft Carter finds Dejah Thoris and  
one remaining crewman at bay, fending off the Green warriors who can  
reach them. Carter leaps in beside her. More Green men are wounded  
and Tars Tarkas, deciding to cut his losses, fires his rifle into the  
air.

TARS TARKAS

ko rinju .i. ko sisti

SUPER: Hold! Stop!

You're thinking too literally.  "Battle" is a happening. Happenings do not
call. People call. It's an idiom. I suggest you attempt to translate the
spirit of the idiom, which is how I arrived at ".ei damba", equating "Battle
Calls" with "We are obligated to fight", but "mi bilga lo nu damba" is too
formal to match the spirit.
