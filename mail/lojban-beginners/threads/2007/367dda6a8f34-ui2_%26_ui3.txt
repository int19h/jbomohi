I have to say, while I can understand your example, I'm not sure  
whether it works or no. It certainly parses as you'd think it does.

Usually {za'a} and {ti'e} get attached to the equivalent of English  
clauses. Then the meaning is pretty clear. For example:

	.i za'a ko'a pu tavla la djan.
	I observe that ko'a was talking to John.

	.i ti'e ko'a pu tavla la djan.
	I hear that ko'a was talking to John.

In general, I think the first example is equivalent to {ko'a pu tavla  
za'a la djan.} also.

mu'o mi'e .aleks.
