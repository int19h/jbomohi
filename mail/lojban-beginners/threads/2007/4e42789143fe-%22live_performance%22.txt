Yeah I don't think "cabna" really captures it at all.  If you're not  
talking about the performance being at this very moment, then what  
you're talking about when you say "live" is really something about the  
nature of the venue & (in the case of recordings) the relative lack of  
post-production.

In one sense "tigni" implies a "live" performance, in that it needs to  
have a tertigni, an audience.  The tertigni might not be very large,  
though; I think it would make sense to say that a studio recording is  
"tigni" and that it's "fi" whoever's in the studio and the recording  
devices.

So depending on the context perhaps "gubni tigni" (public performance)?
