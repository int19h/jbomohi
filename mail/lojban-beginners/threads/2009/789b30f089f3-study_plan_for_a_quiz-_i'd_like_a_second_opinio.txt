I'm of the opinion that words like "badri" can be considered as either
an adjective or a verb, respectively corresponding to e.g. the
Japanese "kanashi-i" and "kanashi-mu", to the English "(to be) sad"
and "(to) lament", to the Esperanto "(esti) trista" and "tristi", and
so on (Japanese adjectives does not require a copula like English and
Esperanto do, so they can be truly verb-less predicates). Some time
ago i suggested "za'i badri" and "zu'o badri" as a pseudo adjective
and a pseudo verb that could be used to specify those nat-lang-ish
senses of this otherwise hermaphrodite jbo-predicate (along which i
suggested also that NU be able to take words like "mi" as its x1,
ready for expressions like "mi zu'o badri").

In the same vein, i doubt the perceived necessity for "morsi" to be
restricted to mean the adjectival "to be dead". In fact, the Chinese
"死 (si)" (from which a part of "morsi" comes) does not distinguish
between "to be dead" and "to die" (and i believe it's the same in
other languages like the South American Guarani,).

>
>  da ca co'i morsi (One dies.)
>  da ca ba'o morsi (One is dead.)

That would work if "morsi" meant "dies" (a change of state, goes from
being alive to being dead) rather than "is dead" (a state).

"ba'o morsi" means "has been dead" (i.e., it is no longer dead), not
"has died" (it is no longer dying).

Compare with "sipna", which is more likely than "morsi" to be used
with "ba'o". "ba'o sipna" means "has been asleep", not "has fallen
asleep". "Falls asleep" is "sipybi'o" or "co'a sipna", and "dies" is
"mrobi'o" or "co'a morsi". "co'i sipna" and "co'i morsi" are complete
events of sleeping, or of being dead. The difference is that events of
being dead don't usually ever end, so its unlikely that one would
speak of "ba'o morsi", "having been dead", except in some very special
contexts.

.i do ca jmive gi'e ba bo morsi
you live now, and you will die

.i sei ko morji se'u go'e
remember! you live, and you die

.i jmive gi'e ku'i ba'a morsi
you live, however you will die

.i ja'e gi jmive co virnu gi djuno lo du'u ba morsi
you live bravely because you know that you will die

.i lo ka virnu cu jicmu lo nu do jmive
bravery is the standard by which you live

.i lo kamvri cu jicmu lo do nunji'e
bravery is the standard by which you live (2)

.i do genai jmive gi zasti
you do not live, you exist

.i do virnu gu'enai jmive gi ba'e to'e jmive
you don't live bravely, you do the opposite of living bravely!

.i ko zo'u virnu jmive ja'e lo je'u nu virnu morsi
live bravely! and you will indeed die bravely.

.i ko'a ba morsi .i ku'i ko'e ba co'u zasti
that one will die, however that one (2) will cease to exist

.i .e'u pilno le nunji'e le banli
use your life for great things!

.i naku lo nunmro cu nu to'e jmive .i ja'aku lo nunza'i cu go'i
(or: .i nu to'e jmive fa genai lo nunmro gi lo nunza'i )
death is not the opposite of living, existing is the opposite of living
