On Sun, May 31, 2009 at 6:02 PM, tijlan <jbotijlan@gmail.com> wrote:
>
> What is the rationale for permitting {me da}, which as a whole is a
> selbri, before {moi}?

I think the story goes something like this: First there was "number
MOI". Then this was extended to "lerfu-string MOI", because lerfu
strings can be used in many places where numbers can be used. But then
lerfu-string is also a sumti, and so they wanted to be able to convert
any sumti with MOI too. The problem is that "sumti MOI" by itself
would make the grammar not LALR1 and this was considered a problem.
Since they already had "ME sumti /MEhU/" as the conversion of sumti
into a tanru-unit, they added "ME sumti /MEhU/ MOI" as well. At least
that's what I think happened.

I would translate them as:

da me lo broda moi
"Something is broda's."

lo me lo broda moi cu brode
"The broda's is/are brode."

> Is this
> construct in the CLL?  If so, where?)

Ch 18. Sect 11:

<<
It is perfectly possible to use non-numerical sumti after ``me'' and
before a member of MOI, producing strange results indeed:


11.15)  le nu mi nolraitru
        cu me le'e snime bolci
        be vi la xel. cu'o
    The event-of me being-a-nobly-superlative-ruler
        has-the-stereotypical snow type-of-ball
        at Hell probability.
    I have a snowball's chance in Hell of being king.
>>

The use of "me (sumti) moi" to mean "x1 is (sumti)'s" is not mentioned
in CLL however, it is a latter innovation.

To me, "ti se ponse mi" sometimes sound too agentive and also
inadequate for such a sense that the x1 (ti) is _among_ other things
that I possess. "ti me mi moi" is less agentive and would imply that
there are other members of "mi's", which is what I sometimes come
across to translate. In the same vein, constructs like "ko'a me lo
tsali me'u moi" appears to me at least semantically useful if not
syntactically economical.
