I think "se la'u", because "li so'o" is the se klani, not the klani.

>  ti fo li so'o nandu zmadu ta

I would say the ve zmadu is the difference in difficulty between ti
and ta, rather than the quotient.

>  ti me li so'o nandu zmadu ta
>
> I also ventured
>
>  ti nu'a pi'i li so'o nandu semau ta
>
> But this one jbofi'e won't parse. Whai is the problem?

"nu'a pi'i" is a tanru unit, so what you have is like "ti broda li
so'o nandu semau ta"

You need "ti nu'a pi'i be li so'o nandu" or "ti fi'o nu'a pi'i li so'o
nandu", depending on what you meant.

mu'o mi'e xorxes

> ti fo li so'o nandu zmadu ta

when you do it like this doesn't this come to "ti (x1) fo li so'o (x4) nandu
zmadu ta (x5)"?  Which doesn't seem to make sense since zmadu doesn't have
an x5.  This happens because when using a selma'o FA every place that comes
after that is incrimenting from the last FA that you used eg. "da klama fi
de di do" has the meaning "da(x1) comes/goes from origin de(x3) via route
do(x4)" completely skipping over the x2 place.

According to jbofi'e, "ta" in my example is x2. This probably has to
do with the fact that the main selbri intervenes between the two
sumti. When it's other than the selbri, the latter sumti is counted
consecutively e.g. in "da fo de ca ku di broda", "di" is x5.

This has been mentioned on the lists before as a bug in jbofi'e.  The main
{selbri} should only interfere with the order of the places when it is not
preceded by any {sumti}, so "{ta}" in "{ti fo li so'o nandu zmadu ta}" does in
fact occupy the x5 place.
