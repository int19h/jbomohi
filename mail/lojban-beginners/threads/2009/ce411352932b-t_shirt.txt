"bu" converts any word into a lerfu, but "ty" already is the lerfu T,
so you don't need to convert it.  "ty bu" should be some other lerfu
related to T, but not T itself, like "ky bu" is Q, or "vy bu" is W.

> Maybe malglico, but is this at least a valid lujvo?

Neither "tybusycreka" nor "tycreka" are valid lujvo, the leading "ty"
will fall off, or if preceded by a CV, it will form a different lujvo:
"lo tybusycreka" = "lotybusycreka" = bloti zei bu zei creka".

You want "ty zei creka" in order to form a lujvo from "ty" and
"creka", because "ty" doesn't have a rafsi.

No.  "{ty.}" does not have any {rafsi}, and it cannot be used as a {rafsi} for
itself.  In addition, according to the CLL, "{ty. bu}" is actually the name for
thorn, not T, which would be plain "{ty.}."  The proper way to make this
{lujvo} is with "{zei}": "{ty. zei creka}."

I'm not really sure what would be a good, non-{malglico} translation of
"T-shirt," though; the best I can come up with is "{torcreka}."

No, a necessary criterion of a valid lujvo is that it only consists of valid rafsi and "hyphens" (ie. y/r/n/l where it is mandated by the rules). Not all cmavo have rafsi, and this includes {ty}. So you can't have {ty} in a "proper" lujvo.

But there is hope! There is a cmavo, {zei} which can be used to combine any series of words into a lujvo. So {ty} + {creka} would yield:

   ty. zei creka

See CLL chapter 4 section 6: http://jbotcan.org/cllc/c4/s6.html
(Unavailable at the time of writing, but Google has it cached: http://74.125.77.132/search?q=cache:DajSLjJkyj4J:jbotcan.org/cllc/c4/s6.html)
