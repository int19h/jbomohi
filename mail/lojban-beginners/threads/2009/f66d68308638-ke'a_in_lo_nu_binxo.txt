Hi.  So as suggested in a thread from a couple days ago, I've started
reading lo nu binxo (the metamorphosis by franz kafka).  I came upon this
passage and had a question:

.... .i lo vrici bukpu mupli to lo nu litru vecnu cu jibri la .samsas. toi
cu kuspe cpana lo jubme noi ga'u ke'a dandu fa lo pixra poi gy ke'a pu za
katna vimcu lo karni gi'e punji lo melbi....

the part that has me confused is this.  We have:  <stuff 1> noi <stuff 2>
ke'a <stuff 3> poi <stuff 4> ke'a <more stuff>

So we have a poi inside of a noi, in this situation what exactly is the
inner-most ke'a referring to?  Is it possible to refer to the outermost
"relativised it" from within a double nested relative clause?

{lo pixra}. (You could probably guess it from context, since it
wouldn't make much sense for it to be {lo jubme}.)

> Is it possible to refer to the outermost
> "relativised it" from within a double nested relative clause?

It's possible, yes. One way is to use {ke'a goi ko'a} in the outer
clause, and then use {ko'a} in the inner clause. That's probably the
least confusing way. Another way is to use a subscript, but I never
remember the exact convention, either {ke'a xi pa} or {ke'a xi re}.
