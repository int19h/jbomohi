I don't know where you're getting that from.  That syntax doesn't even make any
sense (it seems that someone doesn't know what '|' means), and COI is a free
modifier, not an indicator or attitudinal.  According to the Yacc & BNF
grammars in the CLL, an indicator (superset of attitudinal) is:

    (UI | CAI) [NAI]
     | Y
     | DAhO
     | FUhO

> Based on that, I might interpret
> 
>  .ue ro'o .ui ro'i
> 
> as
> 
>  [ UI1 | - | - | - | UI4 | - | - | - ] + [ UI1 | - | - | - | UI4 | - | - | - ]
> 
> But what about
> 
>  .ue .ui ro'i

They're just UI + UI + UI + UI and UI + UI + UI.  The only grouping of
attitudinals & indicators that takes place is that UI and CAI absorb following
NAIs, and then a series of one or more indicators is reduced into a single
token.  Sub-{selma'o} are semantic divisions only and have no effect on
parsing.

In the notation used by CLL for the formal EBNF, that would be:

 ((UI1 | UI2 | UI3) & NAI & CAI & NAI & (UI4 | UI5) & NAI & CAI & NAI) ...

But CLL is wrong about one thing: "with each listed part optionally
present or absent without affecting grammaticality" is not correct.
You can't have NAI by itself, NAI needs something (other than another
NAI) in front of it. "nai nai" for example is ungrammatical. (I think
NAI should be merged with CAI, but that's another story.)

The actual "internal grammar" intended would have to be something like:

 ((UI1 | UI2 | UI3) [NAI] & CAI [NAI] & (UI4 | UI5) [NAI] & CAI [NAI]) ...

but as you noted it would still have some ambiguities. In the official
grammar there is no internal grouping at all, other than for the NAIs.
