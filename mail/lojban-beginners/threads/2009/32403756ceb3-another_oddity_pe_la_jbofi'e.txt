Looking at the official grammar (and confirming this with the official parser),
I don't think that's a bug in jbofi'e.  It appears that a bare {lerfu} string
cannot have any free modifiers attached to it (though KOhA {cmavo} can), so in
order for the above sentence to work, you would have to explicitly terminate
"{my.}" with "{boi}," the "{boi}" being the part that is actually
(syntactically) modified by "{sei}."

"so'uroi" is a tense marker, not a free modifier. It can act as a preposition 
like other tense markers. So "so'uroi le sutra zgana" is a term, as 
is "so'uroiku", but "so'uroi" just before "sutra zgana" modifies it as a 
tense marker.

Pierre

"su'oroi" is a tag.

The free modifiers are:

TO text /TOI/
SEI terms [CU] selbri /SEhU/
SOI sumti [sumti] /SEhU/
(number | lerfu-string) /MAI/
XI (number /BOI/ | lerfu-string /BOI/ | VEI mex /VEhO/)
((COI [NAI]) ... & DOI) (sumti | [relative-clauses] (selbri | CMENE
...) [relative-clauses]) /DOhU/

That appears to be Yet Another jbofi'e Bug (someone should be writing these
down).  The official parser accepts both of those without complaint, along with
plain "{da do'e broda}," though "{da cu do'e do'e broda}" is patently against
the grammar (tags can't come between "{cu}" and the {selbri}).
