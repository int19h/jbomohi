Based on the lojban dictionary I don't quite get the subtle difference
between di'o and tu'i.  Can anyone expound on this for me?

di'o      BAI      at the locus of                           diklo modal,
1st place                            at the locus of ...
tu'i      BAI      associated with site                      stuzi modal,
1st place (used to situate letters)  associated with site ... ; label with
location

It seems like they are similar but I don't really know what the exact
definition of "locus" is anyway so maybe they're completely different.

If you wanted to say "where is John?" would you use di'o, tu'i, or something
completely different?

Neither of those sentences has a predicate. They mean "What at the bathroom" 
and "What in the book's area", or something like that.

I'd say "lo .arpitano cu diklo la .aostas. lo stici rontu'a" but "la kurmaier. 
se stuzi la .aostas." Arpitan is a language, and moves around with its 
speakers, but Courmayeur is a town, and can't move around. ("arpitano" has 
the same place structure as "fraso", and can also refer to Arpitan people or 
anything else Arpitan.)

   Remember that modals such as tu'i and di'o modify the relationship in a
bridi, they aren't a relationship themselves.  So you must use the actual
gismu that these modals come from:  "ma stuzi lo vimku'a" and "ma se diklo
lo cukta". Now, on the other hand, you could say something like "tu'i ma mi
ka'e facki fi lo vimku'a" ("In what site can I find the bathroom?", but that
question could be answered legitimately by any place from where you could
see the bathroom, whether or not the bathroom was actually there)  and
"sedi'o ma mi pu tcidu lo cukta"  (where was I reading the book?" (lit.
"location where? I was reading the book")
