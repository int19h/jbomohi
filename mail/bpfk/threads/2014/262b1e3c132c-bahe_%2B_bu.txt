I have a question about the interaction of BAhE and BU.

The BPFK pages for "nonce connectives 
<http://www.lojban.org/tiki/BPFK+Section%3A+Nonce+Connectives>" and "lerfu 
forming cmavo 
<http://www.lojban.org/tiki/BPFK+Section%3A+lerfu+Forming+cmavo>" seem to 
indicate that {ba'e bu} should not form a letteral. "Nonce connectives" 
says this about {ba'e}:

For grammatical purposes, "ba'e" marks the following word but does not 
change its nature, and does not bind with it or form a single unified 
construct in any way.


And "lerfu forming cmavo" says this:

Combines with the previous word to make a Lojban letteral, provided that it 
is not one of the quote cmavo (ZO, ZOI, LOhU, LEhU) or one of the erasure 
cmavo (SI, SA, SU), ZEI, BAhE, or FAhO.  


However, "nonce connectives" also includes this example:

ko pensi me'o depsna bu .e me'o ba'e bu
*Think about "hesitation sound", and the emphasis letter.*


I read "emphasis letter" as a translation of {ba'e bu}, which would seem to 
indicate that it has formed a Lojban letteral. I would also interpret this 
as a "binding" between {ba'e} and {bu} to "form a single unified 
construct". So it would appear that this example is not consistent with the 
definitions. The parsers are also at odds: YACC and jbofihe reject {ba'e 
bu}, and camxes accepts it.

I assume that whatever is true for {ba'e bu} is also true for {za'e bu}, 
but I'm confused by the seemingly contradictory statements that {za'e} is 
"grammatically identical to za'e" and yet "for grammatical purposes, {za'e} 
binds with and marks the following word". It would seem that whatever it 
means to "bind" the following word, it's not a grammatically significant 
operation, since {ba'e} and {za'e} differ in that aspect.

Can anyone shed light on these issues? Thank you!

I believe the short answer is as follows: "When two magic operators are
in conflict, the leftmost operator always wins."

So {zo ba'e bu} is a letteral (because {zo} wins over {ba'e}) and {broda
ba'e bu} is an emphasized letteral (because {ba'e} wins over {bu}).
