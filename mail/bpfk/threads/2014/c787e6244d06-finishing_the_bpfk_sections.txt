Hello ladies and gentlemen.

So here's the deal. I'd be fine with finishing the entire thing on my 
own (though helpers are allowed :P ), it's a very easy but boring task, 
*but* I have a few comments first.

Firstly, I find it weird that so many of the blue sections haven't been 
voted on even though blue indicates that a page is ready for a vote. 
Then again, many of the allegedly complete sections (marked blue) 
contain many mistakes. Should I care to fix every incorrect example 
sentence? What about strange statements in the notes? What about all the 
sections with irregular formatting that don't follow the standard format 
of a cmavo definition (as defined by the template)? Almost all of the 
time is spent on waiting for the tiki to update all those small 
formatting changes. If it weren't for that, I'd be able to define all 
the cmavo within a very short time (let's say 1 or 2 days).

Secondly, there are some pages which have an ongoing vote, with four or 
five votes recorded which are several years old. The current BPFK has no 
actual members right now, as Robin not only called the BPFK dead, but 
also said that anyone could simply declare themselves a member (in other 
words, anyone is the BPFK) This means it's very hard to judge what the 
different votes are worth. Therefore, I would suggest declaring all 
previous votes null and void and to do a new, proper vote with members 
who are *currently* active in the community.

So let's please pick new voting members (otherwise we don't know what 
constitutes a majority) and then vote on the blue sections. If there are 
problems in them, then people can vote no with an explanation of what's 
wrong.

I'd like to see some of the Non-Contact Spatials spelled out a little more
clearly. In particular, I think the intention is that {ri'u ko'a broda}
assumes that {ko'a} has an orientation, and that the event of {broda}-ing
is to {ko'a}'s right, and that the speaker's location and orientation are
irrelevant, but that point is not entirely clear from the definition as
stated. If I'm facing north and you're to the north of me, facing south
back at me, then a dog between us to the east is "to the right of you" from
my point of view, but also "to your left". .i gerku ri'u je'i zu'a do

Same question for {ca'u} and {ti'a}. "In front of X" can mean "between me
and X" ("a shadow passed in front of the moon"), or it could mean "in the
direction that X is facing". Both should be expressible, and the
definitions need to be unambiguously one or the other.

To me it's not about the facing of the tagged sumti. Some things do not 
even have an intrinsic orientation. A tree has no clearly identifyable 
front, yet something can be in front of the tree from a certain point of 
view.

> Same question for {ca'u} and {ti'a}. "In front of X" can mean "between
> me and X" ("a shadow passed in front of the moon"), or it could mean "in
> the direction that X is facing". Both should be expressible, and the
> definitions need to be unambiguously one or the other.

Both are expressible. {ca'u} unambiguously means the former to me, but 
it's not necessarily between X and *me*. It's in front of X as seen from 
a certain frame of reference or reference point.

    lo ctino cu pagre ca'u lo lunra
    "A shadow passed in front of the moon."

This doesn't specify from what direction we're looking at the situation 
(at the moon), but just as with other tenses, this implicit reference 
point tends to be the here and now of the speaker unless context 
suggests otherwise. We can of course be explicit and use {ki}, which 
sets the space-time frame of reference.

    ki mi lo gerku cu denpa ri'u lo tricu
    "From where I am, the dog is waiting to the right of the tree."

This expands directly to:

    lo nu lo gerku cu denpa cu pritu lo tricu mi
    "The event of the dog waiting is to the right of the tree from me."

For all tenses, {broda TENSE ko'e} is {lo nu broda cu TENSE_SELBRI 
ko'e}, and tenses based on ternary predicates which involve a frame of 
reference additionally use {ki} to mark that frame of reference (e.g. 
{za}, {zo'i}, {vi}, {ni'a}, ...).

I agree that clarifying this somehow in the definitions would be useful.

Having given it some thought, I'm now thinking it would be best to include
an explicit mention of an origin in the definitions. So something like

ca'u: indicates that the event is in front of a certain point, that is,

specifies the point that the event is in front of, when viewed from the
origin.

origin. Right now, the best I have is "Implicit origin is supplied by
context, and is often, but not always, the speaker. Explicit origin is
assignable with {ki}."

to the right of a certain point when viewed from the origin. When tagging a
sumti, the sumti specifies the point the event to the right of." with the
same note.

I'm open to other ideas, but I think being explicit about that sort of
thing is useful.
