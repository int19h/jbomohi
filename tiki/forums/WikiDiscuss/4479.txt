Well, the difference seems to be that no matter
how you make an explanation, you seem always to a
pair of substitutions in a frame, so the crucial
thing is to identify the frame(which is in the
main bridi) since the substituends are given
(once the frame is picked). I don't think this
pattern works generally, but maybe it does for
quantities, just as the insertion of events into
the associated predicate does for causal BAI. 
The problem is, of course, that this does nothing
to say what the frame is.  Insisting on putting
the two sustituends next to eachother leave no
room for afterthought: we like the trolley --
more than the bus does.  I don't think we really
want to leave the simple form ambiguous and
certainly not if we don't have somethings almost
as simple that  is clear.  (I suppose the {tu'a
le sorprekarce} version is a calque of "than the
bus does." But of course the object-bus case is
as much a buried abstraction as the subject-bus,
once we introduce that notion into
considerations.)  Which gets us nowhere either.
