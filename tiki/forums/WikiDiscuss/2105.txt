pc:
> Oughtn't {ei} be for laying an ob on someone -- whether authoritatively or
> not -- to match the other forms in the neighborhood?

I think it reduces to that in the case where the second person is the
agent: {ei do klama}, "it ought to be the case that you go", i.e.
"you ought to go", but I think {ei} is more general, as it works
even for non-agentive situations. For example:

  eipei zo ei tutci lo nu te bilga da de
  Oughtn't {ei} be for laying an ob on someone?
