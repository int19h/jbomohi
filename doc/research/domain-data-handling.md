# Domain data handling for a Lojban "librarian" (jbomo'i) — techniques and source availability

*Research date: 2026-08-26. All "observed" claims in Part B were fetched live on that date (WebFetch and curl). Items marked **[inferred]** are my reasoning, not something a source states. Items marked **[stale]** are pre-2025 but still the canonical reference.*

## Part A — Techniques by data shape (state of practice, 2025–2026)

### A1. IRC / chat logs

**What the literature says about granularity.** The clearest 2025 result is SeCom (Pan et al., "On Memory Construction and Retrieval for Personalized Conversational Agents", arXiv 2502.05589, Feb 2025, ICLR 2025): building retrieval units at the level of *topically coherent segments* beat both turn-level and session-level units, and beat summarization-only memory, on LOCOMO/Long-MT-Bench+; they add "compression-based denoising" (LLMLingua-2) on each unit and evaluate their segmenter on DialSeg711/TIAGE/SuperDialSeg. This is the direct answer to "raw turns vs. episodes": segment, then index the segment (optionally compressed), keep the raw turns for display. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05589

**Thread disentanglement.** The canonical IRC dataset is still Kummerfeld et al.'s Ubuntu IRC disentanglement corpus (arXiv 1810.11118) **[stale, 2018]**; DAG-structured LSTMs (2106.09024) and zero-shot self-supervised disentanglement (2110.12646) are the pre-LLM baselines. The 2026 entry is DD-GEPA (Takada & Mori, arXiv 2606.07894, June 2026), which optimises the LLM prompt (task instruction / utterance representation / output instruction) for disentanglement and reports beating hand-crafted prompts. Practical reading: for a #lojban-sized corpus, an LLM pass per day-file with a well-structured prompt is now the pragmatic disentangler; a trained model is unnecessary. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07894

**Industry patterns for Slack/Discord archives (2025).**
- Snyk, "From Slack Threads to Structured Knowledge" (2025-05-27): each Slack thread that contains a question and answer becomes one chunk; Gemini extracts "a clear question and comprehensive answer"; *only the question is embedded*, the answer is stored as metadata; non-Q&A threads are turned into markdown summaries. No quantitative eval. https://snyk.io/articles/from-slack-threads-to-structured-knowledge-implementing-rag-at-snyk/
- Luna, "Contextual RAG for meeting notes and Slack" (2025): LLM-driven segmentation into 300–500-token units, each labelled with *anchors* (entity it is about) and *semantic labels* (risk/decision/status), plus metadata (date, author, thread id); retrieval = classify query → metadata filter → vector search → aggregate. https://withluna.ai/blog/contextual-rag-product-meeting-notes-slack
- Anthropic, "Contextual Retrieval" (2024-09-19) **[stale but foundational]**: prepend a 50–100-token LLM-written context to each chunk before embedding and before BM25; failure@20 drops 35% (embeddings), 49% (+BM25), 67% (+rerank); one-time cost $1.02 per million document tokens with prompt caching. https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/contextual-retrieval

**Raw chunks vs. summarise-then-embed.** Evidence points to *both*, not either: Summary RAG (RST Journal, 2026) reports +16.5% NDCG@100 on NFCorpus and +12.2% on TREC-COVID from document-level summaries used for routing with full text retained for generation, and a 3.1× indexing speedup; the RAGFlow 2025 year-end review (2025-12-22) lists multi-representation indexing (summaries, generated questions, keywords, tree/RAPTOR-style hierarchies) as the mainstream pattern. A March 2026 systematic study of 36 chunking strategies (arXiv 2603.06976) found content-aware "paragraph group" chunking best overall (mean nDCG@5 ≈ 0.459) and naive fixed-size character chunking worst (< 0.244), with larger embedding models still sensitive to bad segmentation. The ECIR-2025 workshop paper "Reconstructing Context" (2504.19754) finds contextual retrieval preserves coherence better than late chunking at higher compute cost. https://www.rstjournal.com/article/21.2026 · https://ragflow.io/blog/rag-review-2025-from-rag-to-context · https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06976 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19754

**Time-aware retrieval.** Metadata date filtering before vector search is table stakes (Unstructured's metadata guide; the HuggingFace "streaming BBC" blog stores Unix timestamps in ChromaDB metadata and answers "what happened 10 minutes ago"). Research variants: TA-RAG splits a query into subject + temporal window and scores semantic × temporal relevance; "Freshness and the Limits of Heuristic Trend Detection in Temporal RAG" (2509.19376) uses a half-life prior; "Chronological Knowledge Retrieval" (2604.14169, Apr 2026) is the closest analogue to our "how did that decision happen" case — RAG over dated meeting minutes producing *explicitly time-annotated* answers so users can follow the chronology of decisions; the ACL 2026 survey "It's High Time" (2505.20243) organises temporal QA by corpus temporality × question temporality × model capability. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14169 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20243 · https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19376 · https://huggingface.co/blog/RakshitAralimatti/streaming-data-rag

**Nick/alias handling and very large logs [inferred from the above plus observed log format].** The lojban.org logs are one plain-text file per day (`YYYY_MM/YYYY_MM_DD.txt`, lines like `2026-08-01 10:21:45 PDT/-0700 <xx_xx> mi clizu'e ...`), ~70 MB for the "all_logs.txt" aggregate. Day-files are a natural outer unit; an LLM segmenter then produces 5–40 episodes per busy day with (start_ts, end_ts, participants, topic, summary). Nick resolution should be a curated table (person → {IRC nicks, e-mail addresses, wiki usernames, Discord handles}) seeded from `-!- x is now known as y` lines and hostmasks and hand-corrected — the same conclusion the KG literature reaches (see A6). Speaker-aware chunking means keeping `<nick>` prefixes inside the chunk text and adding participants to metadata so "what did xorxes say about ..." is filterable.

### A2. Mailing-list archives

**Threading.** The JWZ algorithm (jwz.org/doc/threading.html, 1997/2002) **[stale, canonical]** remains what everyone implements: build the Message-ID / In-Reply-To / References graph, prune empty containers, then group by normalised subject. Python implementations: `akuchling/jwzthreading` (BSD, small, `jwzthreading.py`), `emersion/python-emailthreads` (parses mbox and formats threads), plus stdlib `mailbox` + `email` for mbox → dict → JSON. Perl `Email::Thread` exists if needed. https://www.jwz.org/doc/threading.html · https://github.com/akuchling/jwzthreading · https://github.com/emersion/python-emailthreads

**Archive ingestion tools.** CHAOSS Perceval has backends for `mbox`, `pipermail`, `hyperkitty`, `groupsio` (not MHonArc) and emits one JSON per message. For Mailman 2 pipermail there are decades-old scrapers (planspace 2014; CoryDolphin gist; ArchiveTeam's Mailman page lists wget of `*.txt.gz`). For MHonArc archives (which is what mail.lojban.org actually serves — see Part B) there is no maintained converter; the MHonArc HTML embeds `<!--X-Date: ... -->` comments (verified) and, per MHonArc convention, `X-Message-Id`/`X-Reference` comments **[inferred, not verified on this host]**, so a small scraper can reconstruct headers. https://github.com/chaoss/grimoirelab-perceval · https://wiki.archiveteam.org/index.php?title=Mailman

**Quote and signature stripping.** Still the same two libraries: mailgun/talon (Apache-2.0; regex quotation removal plus an SVM signature classifier trained on ENRON; 51 open issues, maintenance sporadic) and zapier/email-reply-parser (MIT, Python port of GitHub's Ruby parser, 530 stars). Other options: lever/planer, Teamwork/mailstrip (Go), `claw` on PyPI (1.0.0rc3). For 1990s–2000s list mail (Yahoo/eGroups footers, `>`-quoting with interleaved replies) none of these handle interleaved quoting well; the current practice **[inferred]** is: regex-strip signatures/footers, keep `>` lines but tag them as `quoted`, and let the chunk contextualiser (A1) decide what the reply adds. https://github.com/mailgun/talon · https://github.com/zapier/email-reply-parser

**Granularity.** Message-level chunks with thread id, position-in-thread, and JWZ parent in metadata, *plus* a thread-level summary document (participants, positions taken, outcome) is the multi-representation pattern from A1 applied to mail; Snyk's "embed the question, store the answer" variant is a good fit for lojban-beginners. Lensisku (Part B) threads by normalised subject only, which will mis-thread; use JWZ on the raw source.

**Google Groups.** Owners can export a group's messages and membership (Google Groups Help 9975859); non-owners have no export. `icy/google-group-crawler` is marked deprecated. The February 22 2024 change removed *Usenet* posting/viewing of new content only — "non-Usenet content on Google Groups, including all user and organization-created groups" is explicitly unaffected, and pre-2024 Usenet content remains searchable. So groups.google.com/g/lojban is still fully readable but only scrapeable. https://support.google.com/groups/answer/9975859 · https://support.google.com/groups/answer/11036538 · https://github.com/icy/google-group-crawler

### A3. MediaWiki with full revision history

**Getting history.** Three sanctioned routes (MediaWiki Help:Export, Manual:dumpBackup.php, API:Revisions): (1) `Special:Export` with "Include only the current revision" *unchecked* returns full history if `$wgExportAllowHistory` is true, but caps at 100 revisions per page per request (use `offset`/`limit` params per Manual:Parameters_to_Special:Export); (2) `php maintenance/dumpBackup.php --full` needs shell on the server and does not include deleted revisions, users, or files; (3) `api.php?action=query&prop=revisions&rvlimit=max&rvprop=ids|timestamp|user|comment|content&rvslots=main` — limit 500 per call (50 when `rvprop=content`), single page per call, paginate with `rvcontinue`, `rvdir=newer` for oldest-first. pywikibot/mwclient wrap (3). The mw.lojban.org API is open and responds to all of this (Part B). https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Help:Export · https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Manual:DumpBackup.php · https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/API:Revisions

**Storing revisions compactly.** Git-style delta chains (newest full, older as deltas; zlib-packed) are what MediaWiki's own history dumps and the WikiHist.html project (580 M revisions) rely on; the ACM Hypertext 2024 paper "An Efficient Approach to Store and Access Wikipedia's Revision History" analyses online delta compression for exactly this. For a few-thousand-page wiki with 62 k edits the simplest robust choice **[inferred]** is one git repo with one file per page and one commit per revision (author/timestamp/comment preserved), which gives `git log -p`, `git blame`, and stable `oldid ↔ commit` mapping for free. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3648188.3675150 · https://dumps.wikimedia.org/other/mediawiki_history/readme.html

**Indexing "what did this page say in 2005 vs now".** VersionRAG (Huwiler et al., arXiv 2510.08109, Oct 2025) builds a graph of category → document → version → content/change nodes, classifies query intent (specific-version content / list versions / what changed between v_i and v_j), and reaches 90% on VersionQA vs 58% naive RAG and 64% GraphRAG, 60% on *implicit* change detection where baselines score 0–10%, with 97% fewer indexing tokens than GraphRAG. TianPan's "The 'What Changed' Query Is the RAG Question Your Index Can't Answer" (2026-05-02) distils the engineering recipe: keep old embeddings tagged with version + validity window, content-hash chunks so unchanged sections are shared across versions, keep chunk identity stable via structural (section) chunking, route delta-shaped queries to a comparative-synthesis prompt that outputs added/removed/rephrased. Diffs as first-class documents = the "tracked change" nodes of VersionRAG. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08109 · https://github.com/danielhuwiler/versionrag · https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-05-02-rag-cant-answer-what-changed

**Talk pages and migration artifacts [verified for mw.lojban.org].** The Talk namespace is populated (63+ pages on the first API page, including `Talk:BPFK Section: Tense`, `Talk:BPFK Section: Logical Connectives` etc.), so Talk pages must be ingested as discussion sources with their own revision history. Migration: the earliest revision of `BPFK` is 2013-11-13 (user Gleki), `Lojban timeline` 2013-11-04, `The Dot Side` 2014-06-30 by "Conversion script"; the import log only shows 2021 template uploads; searching for "wikispaces" returns 0 hits. The pre-2013 Tiki edit history was *not* imported — it survives only on the still-live tiki.lojban.org (Part B). Page text also carries artifacts from the bulk renames (edit comments like `Text replace - "jbocre: m" to "m"`), which is worth normalising before indexing.

### A4. Versioned reference documents (CLL 1.0 / 1.1 / 1.2 drafts)

Nothing beyond A3's VersionRAG/TianPan pattern is specific to books, but the DocBook-in-git situation makes it cheap **[inferred from the observed repo layout]**: sections carry stable identifiers in the built HTML (`cll_v1.1_xhtml-section-chunks/section-*.html`) and in DocBook `xml:id`s, so the recommended design is: embed each section *once per edition* with metadata `{edition, section_id, section_number, title, git_commit}`, share chunks across editions by content hash (unchanged sections in 1.1 vs 1.2 are the same chunk with two edition tags), and additionally index a generated "change note" document per (section, edition_a → edition_b) pair produced by `git diff` on the DocBook plus an LLM rewrite ("what changed in 9.3 between 1.0 and 1.1: …"). The agent's "what changed in section X between editions" then hits the change-note index first and falls back to on-the-fly diff of the two section chunks. The CHANGELOG (3.3 KB) at lojban.org/publications/cll/CHANGELOG and the repo's per-tag builds (v1.1-2018-05-21, v1.1-2019-11-14, geklojban-1.2.12…15) supply edition boundaries. Alignment across editions is trivial where section ids are stable and needs a heading-similarity match where they were renumbered.

### A5. Dictionary with change history

**Model.** Standard bitemporal modelling (valid time = when the definition was the community's accepted one; transaction time = when the record was written) is the right frame; the 2025 temporal-RAG literature (TimeR4, TimeRAG, IA-RAG 2606.06044 using Allen interval algebra) all reduce "as-of" questions to interval filters on exactly these two axes. Practically **[inferred]**: one row per definition *version* with `(definition_id, version_id, author, timestamp, text, notes, score_at_time, superseded_by)`, an "as-of" view that picks the highest-scored version valid at date *t*, and embeddings per version (definitions are short, so versions are cheap to embed). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06044 · https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/bitemporal-modeling

**What the Lojban data actually exposes (Part B).** Lensisku's API has `/api/versions/{definition_id}/history`, `/api/versions/diff?from_version=&to_version=`, `/api/versions/{version_id}/version` (all require bearer auth), a public `/api/jbovlaste/changes` cursor feed whose items include a `diff.old_content/new_content` object, per-valsi/per-definition comment threads (`/api/comments/thread?valsi_id=…&definition_id=…`), and `/api/waves/search` across comments *and* the mailing-list archive — i.e., the linkage "definition → discussion that motivated it" is partially materialised already. The legacy jbovlaste XML export omits examples, etymology, votes and discussion fields (per its own export page) and is now behind login/reCAPTCHA, so lensisku's `/api/export/dictionary/{lang}?format=json&positive_scores_only=false` (auth) or the cached exports are the way to get *all* definitions, not just winners.

### A6. Temporal knowledge graphs, timelines, entity resolution

**Graphiti/Zep.** "Zep: A Temporal Knowledge Graph Architecture for Agent Memory" (arXiv 2501.13956, Jan 2025): episodes (text or JSON) → entity/edge extraction → bi-temporal facts (valid-from/valid-to plus ingestion time; old facts invalidated, not deleted) → hybrid retrieval (embeddings + BM25 + graph traversal); custom entity/edge ontologies as Pydantic models; dedup by exact-normalised match then LLM comparison. The self-hosted "Zep Community Edition" was deprecated (April 2025); today's options are Zep Cloud or self-hosting Graphiti (Neo4j ≥ 5.26, FalkorDB, Neptune; Kuzu deprecated). A March 2026 evaluation ("Should the Knowledge Flywheel Use It?") and Graphiti issue #1193 both flag that each episode triggers several LLM + embedding calls (node extraction, edge extraction, dedup) and that "at scale this becomes very expensive". https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956 · https://github.com/getzep/graphiti · https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/03/30/graphiti-agent-memory-store/

**Microsoft GraphRAG and costs.** Pipeline: TextUnits → entity/relationship/claim extraction → Leiden communities → bottom-up community reports; query modes Global (community reports), Local, DRIFT, Basic. Costs reported in 2025–2026 sources: ~$20–40 per 1 M tokens indexed with gpt-4o (Graph Praxis, "The GraphRAG Cost Cliff", 2025); a 500-page corpus (~1.3 MB, ~350 k tokens) costs $50–200 and ~45 min on GraphRAG vs ~$0.50 / 3 min on LightRAG vs < $5 on plain vector RAG (paperclipped.de, 2026-03-22); LightRAG is characterised as 70–90% of GraphRAG quality at ~1/100 the cost; entity extraction is ~58% of GraphRAG's indexing tokens; RAGFlow's 2025 review says GraphRAG consumes "several to dozens of times more tokens than the original text" and that auto-extracted entities are noisy. LazyGraphRAG (Microsoft Research, 2024-11-25) has indexing cost "identical to vector RAG and 0.1% of full GraphRAG" and 700× lower global-query cost, by deferring summarisation to query time. **Per-MB translation [inferred]**: 1 MB of English text ≈ 250 k tokens → full GraphRAG ≈ $5–10/MB at gpt-4o prices, so a 300 MB corpus ≈ $1.5 k–3 k per full re-index (and re-indexing is the norm after prompt tuning); LightRAG ≈ $0.05–0.10/MB; Graphiti sits between, dominated by per-episode call count rather than tokens. https://microsoft.github.io/graphrag/ · https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/ · https://www.paperclipped.de/en/blog/graph-rag-production/ · https://medium.com/graph-praxis/the-graphrag-cost-cliff-how-33-000-became-33-in-eighteen-months-be1b0fbe37e4

**Entity/alias resolution.** Every 2025–2026 source agrees this must precede graph construction: Zingg's "Before You Build a Knowledge Graph, You Need to Solve Identity" (2026-06-12) — a 240 k-entity graph that should have had 140 k, and "in an agentic AI workflow that [human review] loop is broken"; LINK-KG (2510.26486) does chunk-wise LLM coreference with an alias-to-canonical map carried across chunks; CORE-KG (2506.21607) similar; the ODSC/TDS "entity-resolved knowledge graphs" pieces make the same point for GraphRAG. Automatic KG builders "consistently produce duplicate entities" across long contexts. For a community where the same person is `lojbab`/Bob LeChevalier/`lojbab@lojban.org`, `xorxes`/Jorge Llambías, `rlpowell`/Robin Lee Powell, `la gleki`/Gleki, the registry is small (low hundreds of people) and should be curated by hand with an LLM proposing candidates. https://www.learningfromdata.zingg.ai/p/before-you-build-a-knowledge-graph · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26486

**Curated timeline + registry vs automated KG for ~300 MB [inferred, but strongly supported].** Given (a) VersionRAG beating GraphRAG 90% vs 64% on version questions with 97% fewer tokens, (b) LazyGraphRAG matching GraphRAG global quality at vector-RAG indexing cost, (c) the noise/duplicate-entity findings, and (d) the existence of a hand-built 200-entry `Lojban timeline` wiki page (1955–2024) and 38 LLG annual-meeting minutes/transcripts pages, the cost-effective design is: a curated event timeline (each event: date, actors, sources, outcome, status such as "ratified by LLG members 2002-12") + a curated entity registry + ordinary hybrid RAG with contextual chunks and time metadata; add a lightweight automatically-extracted "event candidates" layer (LLM over thread summaries and IRC episodes proposing dated events with citations) that humans promote into the timeline. Full GraphRAG community reports are only worth running if "what are the main themes of 2003–2005 BPFK discussion" style global questions turn out to matter, and then LazyGraphRAG-style query-time summarisation is the cheaper route.

### A7. Provenance, citation, quote-checking, multi-view answers

**Stable identifiers available in this corpus [verified formats, mapping inferred].** Mail: RFC `Message-ID` (present in the old_lojban-list raw files and in lensisku's `message_id` field; MHonArc pages expose it via the msgNNNNN.html number plus embedded header comments). IRC: `(channel, YYYY-MM-DD, HH:MM:SS, nick)` from the lojban.org log format, which is unique enough to serve as a citation and to deep-link to a day-file. Wiki: `revid`/`oldid` from the API (`?oldid=NNN` URLs are permanent). CLL: `section-*.html` anchors in the 1.1 section-chunk build plus the git tag. Dictionary: `definitionid` and lensisku `version_id`. BPFK: wiki page + oldid (votes were wiki polls "tied to pages generally rather than specific versions", per the obsolete BPFK Procedures page — so the *page revision at vote-close time* is the only reliable anchor).

**Citation reliability findings (2025–2026).** "Cited but Not Verified" (arXiv 2605.06635, May 2026) parses inline citations from 14 models' reports and checks Link Works / Relevant Content / Fact Check: strong models keep links > 94% valid and > 80% relevant but only 39–77% *factually* accurate, and accuracy falls ~42% as tool calls scale from 2 to 150 — "more retrieval does not produce more accurate citations". VeriCite (SIGIR-AP 2025) adds rigorous post-hoc verification of each citation; "Relevant Is Not Warranted" (2605.28044) calibrates *evidence force* (a retrieved passage being on-topic is not the same as it supporting the claim); "Detecting and Correcting Reference Hallucinations" (2604.03173) reports 11–57% citation hallucination in deployed systems; HalluCiteChecker (2604.26835) and CiteCheck (2605.27700) are lightweight checkers for bibliographic citations. Span-level groundedness detectors (LettuceDetect, MiniCheck, RAGTruth-trained classifiers) verify sentences against supplied context. The verbatim approach — ACL-Verbatim / VerbatimRAG (2605.21102, May 2026) — sidesteps hallucination by answering with *extracted exact spans*; a 150 M-param ModernBERT extractor beat LLM extractors (53.6 vs 48.7 word-F1). **Design consequence [inferred]:** every quotation the librarian emits must be checked by exact/normalised substring match against the cited source chunk before the message is sent; failing quotes are dropped or re-fetched, never paraphrased into existence. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06635 · https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3767695.3769505 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.28044 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21102

**Rendering in Discord [known platform facts, not re-verified].** Masked links (`[text](url)`) render only inside embed descriptions/field values, not in plain message content; link buttons (message components) are the other clickable option; message jump links have the form `https://discord.com/channels/{guild}/{channel}/{message}`; 2000-char content / 4096-char embed-description limits push toward numbered footnotes with a compact source list in an embed. https://discordjs.guide/legacy/popular-topics/embeds

**Multi-view answers for disputes.** "DRAGged into Conflicts" (Cattan et al., arXiv 2506.08500, June 2025) gives a taxonomy of knowledge-conflict types with the *desired* behaviour per type and the CONFLICTS benchmark; its key finding is that prompting the model to explicitly reason about potential conflicts among retrieved documents "significantly improves the quality and appropriateness" of responses. "RAG Must Move Beyond Factual Grounding to Represent Diverse Opinions" (2604.12138, Apr–Jul 2026) shows plain retrieval silently drops minority positions before the LLM sees them and proposes opinion extraction + entity-linked opinion metadata, with human raters preferring the opinion-enriched answers 79.2% of the time. "The Deliberative Illusion" (2606.03032) documents stance homogenisation in multi-agent deliberation — a warning against "debate" architectures for presenting disputes. "Conflicts in Texts" (2504.19472) is a survey. **Pattern [inferred]:** retrieve per-position (cluster hits by stance/author before truncation, as the opinion-aware paper does), require the answer to list positions with holder + date + citation, state what is *ratified* (and by which body, with the ratifying document cited) separately from what is *argued*, and evaluate with a small hand-built set of known Lojban disputes (dot-side, `.i`-less `xu`, `ce'u`, `zo'e` scope, cmevla morphology) checking each known position is present and correctly attributed. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08500 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12138 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03032

## Implications for jbomo'i design

1. **Acquisition plan is now concrete.** Wiki: full-history via `api.php prop=revisions` (open, 500/call) into a git repo, plus a one-off scrape of tiki.lojban.org page histories for 2001–2013. Mail: scrape MHonArc (`msgNNNNN.html` + embedded header comments) for 22 public lists, take `old_lojban-list/` as raw RFC-822, and treat lensisku's mail API as a cross-check for Google-Groups-era messages; `llg-members`/`llg-board` need credentials or a member's mbox. IRC: pull `irclogs.zip` + monthly dirs; it is already ~70 MB of text. Dictionary: lensisku JSON exports with `positive_scores_only=false` plus the public `changes` feed; per-definition version history needs an API token. CLL: clone lojban/cll, build/scrape section-level HTML per tag.

2. **Index shape: multi-representation with time everywhere.** Every chunk carries `{source_type, source_id, timestamp/valid_from, valid_to, author_canonical, edition/revid}`. Raw chunks (message, IRC episode, wiki section @ revision, CLL section @ edition, definition @ version) get an Anthropic-style contextual prefix; each thread / IRC episode / wiki-page-history / definition-history additionally gets a summary document. Retrieval = intent classify → metadata filter (date window, source, person) → hybrid dense+BM25 → rerank, following SeCom, Luna, and VersionRAG rather than GraphRAG.

3. **"What changed" is a first-class query type.** Implement VersionRAG-style change nodes for wiki pages, CLL sections, and definitions: content-hash chunks shared across versions, LLM-written change notes per (unit, v_a→v_b), and a comparative-synthesis prompt. This answers "what did the BPFK Section on tense say before the 2020 edit" and "what changed in 9.3 between 1.0 and 1.1" without diffing at query time.

4. **Curated timeline + entity registry instead of an automated KG.** Seed from the `Lojban timeline` page, the 38 LLG minutes pages, BPFK checkpoint states, CLL CHANGELOG/tags, and lensisku's change feed; have an LLM propose dated "event candidates" from thread and episode summaries, humans promote. Entity registry maps people across nick/e-mail/wiki/Discord identities and is used at both index time (canonical author metadata) and query time. Reserve LazyGraphRAG-style query-time community summarisation for global "themes" questions; skip full GraphRAG indexing (est. $1.5–3 k per pass at 300 MB, noisy entities).

5. **Ratification status is data, not prose.** Model each normative claim as `{claim, status ∈ {baseline-1997, baseline-2002, BPFK-checkpointed, BPFK-voted-not-checkpointed, LFK, unofficial-1.2, proposal}, ratifying_body, ratifying_document_id (oldid / message-id / CLL section), date}`. The Baseline Statement, BPFK Procedures ("passed proposals define Baseline LLG Lojban"), BPFK Sections colour codes, LLG minutes, and the "geklojban ≠ LLG" disclaimer supply the vocabulary. Answers must separate "ratified by X on date D [cite]" from "argued by Y [cite]".

6. **Citations are verified before sending.** Emit citations as stable ids (Message-ID; `#chan YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS nick`; `mw oldid=N §heading`; `CLL 1.1 §9.3 (tag v1.1-2019-11-14)`; `lensisku definition N version M`) and run a verbatim substring check of every quoted span against the cited chunk (VerbatimRAG/ACL-Verbatim logic; the 39–77% fact-accuracy numbers from 2605.06635 are the reason). In Discord, render numbered footnotes with an embed carrying masked links or link buttons.

7. **Disputes get a multi-view template.** Retrieve per position (cluster by stance/author before truncation), prompt for explicit conflict reasoning (DRAGged into Conflicts), output "Positions: … / Ratified: … / Open: …" with attribution and dates, and evaluate against a hand-built set of known Lojban disputes (dot-side, cmevla morphology, `ce'u`, `zo'e` scope, `xorlo`) — checking presence and attribution of each known position, not just answer correctness.

8. **Watch-outs.** MHonArc numbering is not chronological (sort by X-Date, not msg number); the lojban-list archive has at least one epoch-1970 date artefact; IRC logs may be Lojban-filtered (verify by sampling English-heavy days); jbovlaste XML lacks votes/etymology; lensisku threads by subject (use JWZ on raw headers instead); the wiki's bulk "Text replace" edits in 2014 are noise to skip in change detection; Google Groups is view-only for non-owners.
