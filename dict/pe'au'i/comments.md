## 2026-07-28T18:04:12Z — bairyn (comment 5064, on definition 75322, in reply to 5062)



{tu'a} means ‘abstraction that contains this sumti’. Sometimes I don't want an abstraction. {zo'ei} exists for some uses of {tu'a} that allows for there to not be an abstraction; {zo'ei} seems more general. {zo'ei} is closely related to {pe'au'i}, but I think {pe'au'i} is a little more specific. They also have different grammar: LAhE like {zo'ei} converts the sumti, and UI3c like {pe'au'i} are attitudinals, and perhaps this set of UI3c based on {pe'a} may be thought of as different ways of interpreting a construct non-literally - or alternatively conveniently explicitly specifying ways in which it is literal.

​

But I think it'd be good to add {zo'ei} and {tu'a} on the list of words to see; I'll add that in the notes.

​

(I suppose you could argue that technically the type of {su'u} involved with a {tu'a} can be one that extracts a concrete related sumti, not a traditional abstraction, and then {tu'a} could work more generally. Even then, it's still handy to have constructs that don't emphasize or connote traditional abstractions like «lonu». Personally, I normally don't use {tu'a} when I don't want an abstraction, even if you might be able to shoehorn an interpretation that technically works in there. Normally I see {zo'ei} as a more correct choice in this case.)
