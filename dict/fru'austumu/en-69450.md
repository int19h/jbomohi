$x_1$ is a frustum/cone/cylinder/pyramid made of material $x_2$ with larger base $x_3$ and at-most-as-large base $x_4$, with other characteristics $x_5$.

## Notes

$x_4$ cannot be larger in hyperarea than $x_3$. A cone and a pyramid are degenerate frusta with exactly one base ($x_4$) being exactly a point (the apex); a line segment is a doubly-degenerate frustum with $x_3$ (and therefore $x_4$) being a point. The bases should typically be approximately parallel to one another; they need not be perpendicular to their axis. Except possibly when at least one base is a point, they will be (apprpximately) geometrically similar to one another (having the same cross-section along parallel planes wrt the axis, modulo scale/size, which will nonetheless be multiplied by a uniform factor in all 'dimensions'). A round base will produce, approximately, a circular cone or a (circular) cylinder.
