## 2026-03-22T04:33:26Z — bairyn (comment 4002, on definition 74914, in reply to 4001)



I see you downvoted these definitions. I'll grant perhaps «to'eki'u» could be interpreted as being equivalent. The reason is that CLL 9.13 says that na'eki'u does not mean fi'o na'e krinu, but that the modal is something other than «krinu», i.e. something not «krinu» (e.g. mukti). It does not say what «to'e» means, but I suppose it'd be reasonable to interpret it as fi'o to'e krinu, but technically maybe there's some ‘opposite’ relationship that's different from «to'e krinu». Still, in that case, then «ki'u'oi» could be an alias for «to'eki'u», saving a syllable at the expense of a new cmavo.

​

I'll revise these definitions to mention that «to'eki'u» is likely interpretable to mean the same thing. Why did you downvote?
